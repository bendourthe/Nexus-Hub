## The ambiguity, and the call made on it

The review asked to double "'Vague', 'Engineered' and other similar text items on the Foundations page". Two kinds of small uppercase label live there, and "similar" does not obviously cover both.

A **block tag** names a whole box or figure: Vague, Engineered, the budget tags, the boundary tag, and the legend terms the review pointed at in its own screenshot. Those are doubled.

An **in-cell descriptor** sits inside a quarter-width matrix cell, like `Project folder or workspace` in the Context Engineering material list. Those keep their size. Doubling a 10.5px label inside a 250px cell does not make it easier to read; it makes the cell three lines tall, which works directly against the legibility this review round is about.

The decision is asserted in both directions, so the in-cell sizes read as a choice rather than as something missed. If the operator wants them doubled too, the test says where to change the decision first.

## Eight numbers became one token

The eleven rules touched were declaring `11px`, `11.5px`, or `12px` by hand. Doubled, those are 22px, 23px, and 24px, differences nobody can see on an uppercase tag. They all now resolve to `--fx-tag: 23px`, because a ninth hand-authored size is how a design system stops being one.

Each repoint asserted the literal declaration it was replacing, which caught a real mismatch immediately: `.fx-tokfig-cap` is selected as `.fx-copy .fx-tokfig-cap` and the captions live in `.fx-diagram`.

## A dead rule, found by measuring

That mismatch turned out to be a pre-existing defect. The rule has matched nothing since the token figure moved out of `.fx-copy`, so both captions have been rendering as plain 16px sentence-case body text with no letter spacing and no uppercase.

That is exactly what the review's screenshot 4 is complaining about when it asks for one of them to be "styled like 'Vague'": it is asking for the styling the rule already describes and never applied. Phase 4 fixes it, because Phase 4 is the phase that renames those captions.

Worth noting what could not have caught this. The class-coverage guard from v4.4.3 proves every class used in the markup has a style rule. It says nothing about whether a rule's selector matches anything. Those are different questions, and only one of them was being asked.
