## What was wrong with it

Two columns of prose, four labelled sentences each. Nothing in it was inaccurate, and that is the
point: the reader had to hold eight sentences in their head and do the comparison themselves. The
review asked for a clear illustration of what a chatbot does and can do against what an agentic
platform does and can do.

## The idea the rebuild is built on

Both lanes show the same two zones, the conversation window and the work surface. The only thing
that changes between the lanes is which zones the lane can reach. That is the argument of the section
rendered as a picture, and it is why the test asserts the zone names are IDENTICAL between the lanes:
a rebuild that gave each lane its own zones would look tidier and teach nothing.

The chatbot lane reaches the conversation window and leaves the work surface untouched. The agentic
lane reaches both, and the reached work surface carries three chips naming what changed, which makes
"leaves durable artifacts" concrete instead of asserted. One request box at the top splits into both
lanes through two triangles, so the shared input is visible rather than described.

The four labels the earlier version carried are kept underneath each lane, terser: the picture
answers where, and the words answer what.

## One rule inherited and applied

The unreached zone is marked with a dashed edge and a full-contrast label reading `untouched`. It is
not dimmed. That is the v4.4.2 contrast rule (`BG-13`, `BG-14`) applied to a state marker rather than
to a motion step, and the test measures the worst contrast ratio among all four zone labels against
their own backgrounds, so a later change that fades the inactive one fails.
