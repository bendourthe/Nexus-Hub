## What changed, and why the rings went

v4.4.3 drew the two harnesses as nested rings with a separate six-stop journey below them. That
showed WHERE the layers sit, and it asked the reader to map a picture onto a list. The review asked
for something different: a chart where a prompt goes to a model, the model reasons inside the
platform harness, that layer's limitations are visible, and the Nexus Hub harness comes next adding
specialised skills and security.

So there is one flow now, five steps with a triangle between each:

1. **The request** -- a prompt and the material it names, from the operator.
2. **The model**, like *a powerful brain* -- enormous general capability, and no idea how this
   project works, what it already decided, or what counts as finished here.
3. **The platform harness**, like *a graduate degree* -- competent general practice, built in, with
   its five ports. Its limits are stated in the step: it knows the general practice, not this
   project's standard, and it carries nothing from one session to the next.
4. **The Nexus Hub harness**, like *decades of practice across many fields* -- matched procedures,
   hooks that refuse unsafe actions whatever the prompt said, written gates, and artifacts that
   chain, with its four ports.
5. **What comes out** -- reviewed, verified work and the artifacts that record how it got there.

The analogy is the operator's, and it does real work: it explains why a layer that adds no
intelligence still changes the result. The limits box is what makes the fourth step necessary rather
than decorative, so the test asserts it exists and names the session boundary.
