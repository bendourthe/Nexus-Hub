## What came from the mockup, and what deliberately did not

The valuable part of `generative-ai-model-flow.html` is its **spine**: eight numbered stages grouped so a reader can see that training happens once and long before, that two choices are made when a request arrives, that generation is one mechanism repeated, and that the output kind is a property of the model rather than of the prompt. That spine is now the scene's structure.

Two of its stages the guide genuinely lacked, and both are new here:

- **Stage 03, Select.** Which model, and how much room it may spend. The three effort levels are all visible with the middle one marked typical, and the caveat is stated: effort is a budget for extra work before answering, not an intelligence dial, and not every model offers it.
- **Stage 06, Predict.** A scored candidate list after a real fragment of the running example, with the honest line that the numbers are illustrative and the shape is what is real.

**Stage 05, Tokenize, is one line on purpose.** The Tokens scene two scenes above already carries that figure. A second copy would be the fourth duplication this phase had to remove.

Three of the mockup's constructions did not come:

- **Its two tablists.** Three prompt kinds behind one, three output kinds behind another. A reader comparing three things cannot compare them one at a time.
- **Its stage 07 ring.** Four labels placed around a core by absolute offset, with the arrows drawn in a separate fixed 460x270 viewBox. That is what the operator is describing as misplaced elements and unreadable text, and it cannot be fixed by nudging coordinates, because the labels and the arrows live in two unrelated coordinate systems. The passes are a grid here, and the test measures every pair of boxes for overlap at five widths. That is the assertion that would have caught the mockup.
- **Its select, textarea, and buttons.** Nothing on this page waits to be operated.

## The duplication a screenshot caught and the tests did not

The first run of this rebuild rendered the base-versus-reasoning lanes **twice**: once under Predict and again under Reason. Every test passed.

The cause is one line of markup structure. `.mx-lanes` is a CHILD of `.fx-pass`, not its sibling, so harvesting `.fx-pass` for the Predict stage dragged the lanes along, and placing the lanes under Reason put them on the page a second time. The rebuilt scene was internally consistent, balanced, and complete; it just said everything twice.

Only a screenshot showed it. The script now harvests the token block on its own and rebuilds the `.fx-pass` wrapper around it, and it asserts single occurrence of `.mx-lanes`, of `data-grammar="one-pass"`, and of `.fx-pass-note` before writing.

Two smaller duplications went with it. The provider row's two cards said, verbatim, what stages 01 and 02 now say in their own prose, so the row is dropped and `data-stage="delivery"` moved onto stage 02. And the phase wrapper carried `data-stage="output"` while the harvested tiers block already had it, which gave the document two and tripped the chronology guard.

**The lesson is about what "complete" means in an assertion.** Every structural check passed on a scene that told the reader the same thing twice, because none of them asked whether anything appeared more often than it should. Counting is now part of the harvest.

## The second flow root

The request region was its own `data-flow-root` before the rebuild and briefly stopped being one. That matters beyond a passing test: the connector engine draws within a root, and one root spanning the provider and request regions would draw a line from training to a prompt, which is the exact misconception this scene exists to prevent.
