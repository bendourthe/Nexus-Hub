## What this phase set out to do

Ship the mechanically-detectable figure-defect audit as a bundled script,
measured in screen space, with the two false-positive defences that made the
source project's first version useless.

## Steps

1. Created an isolated worktree from `develop`, immediately after v4.11.0 was released and back-merged.
2. Read `measure_handbook.py` before writing anything, and reused its established idiom rather than inventing a second one: `status: "unverified"` by default, a JSON report with `findings` and `errors`, Chromium with outbound requests blocked and aborted, `document.fonts.ready` before measuring.
3. Wrote `geometric_audit.py` with six checks.
4. Generated nine fixtures: six broken (one defect each), a clean baseline, and the two defences.
5. Ran the matrix. It was wrong in three places. Fixed all three (below).
6. Added `--disable <check>` so the negative control is repeatable rather than a manual ritual.
7. Wrote 19 tests, ran the audit against the real repository handbooks, ran the fast profile.

## Troubleshooting

### `clipped-text` passed when it had to fail

The first matrix run showed the `clipped-text` fixture reporting nothing.

The cause was mine and structural: an SVG defaults to `overflow: hidden`, and
my `visibleRect` helper walks ancestors intersecting the element against
anything that clips. For text inside an SVG, that means intersecting it against
the very viewport whose clipping the check is trying to detect. The result can
never exceed the frame, so `worst` was always <= 0.

Check 1 now measures the raw `getBoundingClientRect` against the SVG's own
rect. Ancestor clipping still matters for the overlap checks, where geometry
clipped away really is invisible, so `visibleRect` stays in use there.

### The clean baseline was not clean

`clean.html` reported `label-on-trace`. Investigating rather than assuming the
check was wrong: the lower trace began at `M40,260` and the tick label `0s` sat
at `y=292` with a 14px font, so the label's box genuinely covered the trace's
first sampled point. A true positive against a fixture I had mislabelled. The
traces moved clear of the axis band.

### The wiggle defence asserted a false positive that was not false

`label-beside-wiggle` reported `label-on-trace`, which is the defence failing.
The path was a zigzag between y=40 and y=280 every 40px in x, so it had ink
nearly everywhere, and the label at (300,160) really was on the line. The
fixture tested nothing.

Redesigned: the path hugs the bottom band (y 255-285) across the full width and
takes one tall spike to y=40 at x=540. Its bounding box still spans x 20-580
and y 40-285, which covers the label, while the nearest ink is roughly 100px
away. A bounding-box test reports a collision here and a sampled test does not,
which is exactly the distinction the defence exists to prove.

### The oversized fixture carried two defects

Scaling the type up pushed its ascender 3px above the viewBox top, so the
fixture tripped `clipped-text` as well. Real, but incidental to what the
fixture is for. Baseline moved from y=12 to y=26.

## What went right

Reading `measure_handbook.py` first meant the new script matched an existing
contract rather than inventing a parallel one, including the rule that an
unavailable renderer reports `unverified` and exits 2 rather than passing.

The "exactly its own check" assertion earned its place immediately: it caught
one genuine bug and two bad fixtures on its first run. A looser assertion -
"the fixture reports something" - would have passed all three.
