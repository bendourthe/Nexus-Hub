# Comparison: Structural AI-Slop Signatures and Nexus-Hub's De-Slop Pipeline

Authored: 2026-09-28
Authoring cycle: v4.13.x (last tag `v4.13.3`)
Adoption target: v4.17.10
Target status: confirmed by the user on 2026-09-28 (free slot after `v4.17.6`, which this adoption must follow; split from the sibling effort comparison, now `v4.17.11`; no renumber)
Sources: one (a local PDF of a research paper)
Skill: `cross-project-comparison`

The source shows that AI-generated commercial writing carries a structural signature (a tidy, self-announcing shape) that survives rewording: after each model rewrote its own posts and replaced 73% of their 13-word sequences, structural detection stayed at 98.1 macro-F1. Nexus-Hub's anti-slop pipeline works almost entirely at word and sentence level, and its deterministic detector skips headings, so a draft it de-slops can keep its AI shape intact. The adoptions below add a document-shape pass that runs before the word-level pass, a countable subset of shape checks to the offline detector, and a claim-preservation gate on Edit mode, while explicitly not adopting the paper's authorship classifier.

The source is a document, so the article strategy (insight extraction) applies. Section numbers refer to the paper.

**Evidence quality and scope.** The study is a replication of StoryScope on 2,250 pre-2023 human B2B blog posts against 11,250 single-pass mirrors from five models, with domain-disjoint splits and a human gold-annotation check (kappa 0.928 human-human, 0.946 human-model). Its claims cover single-pass English blog posts, not documentation, reports, or decks, and not humanizer tools (Section 9). The author operates a commercial GEO product, which the paper discloses. These features describe what AI text tends to look like; they are not evidence that a given text is low quality or machine-written.

## Scope rule for Nexus-Hub's own artifacts

Nexus-Hub mandates fixed structure for plans, comparisons, session histories, known-gaps files, and the End-of-Task Summary. By S4 those templates sit in the common configuration on purpose, because reviewability and machine parsing depend on it. Shape checks therefore apply to prose-first artifacts (READMEs, guides, handbooks, articles, release-note narrative, presentify pages, decks, and chat replies) and do not apply to template-governed records. The template sections are the contract; only the prose inside them is subject to the word-level catalog.

One distinction needs stating in B2b: the Communication Contract's "outcome first" rule is not the thesis-before-content tell. Outcome first states the result in the first sentence ("The build fails because CI is not running"). The tell announces what the document will do ("This guide covers why builds fail, three fixes, and how to measure them"). The first delivers content; the second describes it.

## Step 7: Risks, conflicts, and items not recommended

**Conflicts to resolve during implementation:**

- **Minimum effective edit vs. shape repair.** Removing a roadmap preamble or a restating close is a structural edit that can look large in a diff. It stays within the minimum-edit rule because it deletes meta-text rather than rewriting content; B1's preservation gate is what proves no content left with it.
- **Numbered steps and caveats.** The Communication Contract requires numbered steps and stated risks. S11 lists both as model-specific tells. They are kept: the not-recommended list below rejects per-model tells as editing targets.
- **Advisory, not gating.** Agenda slides, conclusion sections, and headings are legitimate in many genres. B4 findings are `advisory` and never gate; only the existing `defect` class gates.

**Not recommended:**

| Item | Class | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The paper's authorship classifier (XGBoost over 187 LLM-scored features) | `drop-outright` | Needs gated per-post data and trained models, 11 model calls per document to a scoring vendor (MCP Registry Policy: new outbound calls and a new data processor), and it outputs an authorship verdict, which Detect mode forbids. Detection also does not measure quality. |
| Word-level detectors (fine-tuned encoders, likelihood-based zero-shot) | `drop-outright` | Require model downloads, conflicting with the zero-model-download posture, and the paper documents their brittleness (S10). |
| Per-model structural tells as editing targets | `drop-outright` | Several are practices Nexus-Hub mandates (numbered procedures, caveats, baseline disclosure for numbers); penalizing them would degrade documentation. |
| Second-person density and reader-naming metrics as rules | `drop-outright` | Legitimately high in guides and runbooks; any threshold would be degenerate by S6's own test. |
| A rarity score over Nexus-Hub documents | `drop-outright` | Needs a feature space and reference corpus Nexus-Hub does not have, and would reward novelty for its own sake in documents whose templates exist on purpose. |
| Copying instrument questions verbatim from the release | deferred to B8 | License and scan not yet checked; the follow-up comparison decides. |
