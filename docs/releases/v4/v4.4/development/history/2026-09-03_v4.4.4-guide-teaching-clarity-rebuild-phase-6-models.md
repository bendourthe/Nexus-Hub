## What the scene was, and what the review wanted

It catalogued four output kinds and showed a provider timeline. That is not how a model works, and
the review said so. The three things it asked for are all mechanical facts:

1. **Next-token prediction.** The answer builds one token at a time, each chosen with the request,
   the material, and every token already produced in view. Eight chips arrive in sequence, the last
   one marked as the token being chosen, with the caveat that this is the same mechanism running
   again rather than a transcript of hidden reasoning.
2. **Base against reasoning.** Two lanes. The base lane is prompt then answer, one pass. The
   reasoning lane adds a dashed self-prompt node and a loop marker: it drafts an approach, checks it,
   revises, and answers once that settles. The trade-off is stated rather than implied, slower and
   more expensive against better on work that needs a plan first.
3. **Three modality tiers.** Text, multimodal, omni, each naming what goes in and what comes out.
   The approved image sits in the multimodal tier and the approved moving image in the omni tier, so
   both are still hash-matched to the ledger.

## Models named, and one correction to the brief

One family per provider, no version numbers, because a family name ages slowly while a version ages
in weeks and a static offline file cannot refresh itself. Anthropic Claude Fable, OpenAI GPT Sol,
Google Gemini Flash, and xAI Grok, each read from that vendor's own documentation today.

The brief listed the fourth as "Cursor Grok". Grok is xAI's model; Cursor is a platform that offers
models rather than the maker of one. The chip reads `xAI Grok`, and the correction is flagged here
and in the handoff rather than silently applied.

## Two structural repairs, and the trap behind both

Two replacements used a non-greedy regex, which stops at the FIRST matching closer rather than the
matching one. Each left stray closing tags:

- The first orphaned the old effort note, which then repeated what the new copy already said.
- The second closed the request region early. That dropped a nested flow root and cut the connector
  count from seven to two, and it rendered without any visible error.

`test_flow_connectors_never_cross_a_card` caught the second, because it asserts three flow roots and
seven paths. The repair was not more closer-counting: the section is re-emitted from its harvested
parts with the nesting written out, and the script asserts each part is balanced before assembling.
After it: three roots, seven paths, tiers inside the request region.

## One accessibility contract strengthened

Zeroing the transition under reduced motion made a step reveal instantly, but it still waited for the
observer to play the sequence, so a reader who had asked for less motion saw an empty token strip
until they scrolled it into view. A step is now never hidden at all under reduced motion.

## Three contrast defects the strengthened contract exposed

The reduced-motion change had a side effect worth recording. The contrast sweep samples only VISIBLE
text, so every element that stayed invisible until its sequence played had been outside the sweep's
reach. Making them visible put them inside it, and three failed AA in light theme: the guardrails
result pills at 3.92 (shipped in v4.4.3 Phase 2), the comparison change chips at 4.08 (v4.4.3
Phase 7), and this phase's own material examples at 1.90.

The first two are pre-existing and shipped invisible. The third has a cause worth naming: `--blue`
had no light-theme override at all. It had been used for strokes and fills for four versions, where
a bright sky blue is fine, and the moment it carried TEXT its absence mattered. One override added,
and the two tints give way to outlines in light theme.

The lesson is uncomfortable: **a contrast sweep that skips hidden text will pass a palette it never
measured.**
