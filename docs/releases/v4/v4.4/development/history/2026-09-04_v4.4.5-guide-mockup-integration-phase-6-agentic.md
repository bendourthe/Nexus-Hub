## The comparison, before the code

Reading `agentic-platforms-explained.html` against the existing scene showed the scene already carried the mockup's centre: one shared request down two lanes, the four-step loop, one permission boundary, and the two outcomes. So this phase is not a rebuild of what was there. It adds the three things the mockup teaches and the scene did not, and drops the things the mockup teaches twice.

**Added.** The equation, with the mockup's honest gloss: agentic describes what a system DOES, not a special kind of model, and the same model sits in both lanes. That is the most useful sentence in the mockup and the scene was missing it entirely, which left the two lanes looking like two different models.

The boundary's three settings, all visible. In the mockup these are buttons, and its best lesson only fires if the reader presses the read-only one and watches step 03 stop: a boundary REPORTS a refusal rather than routing around it. A lesson that good should not be behind a control, so all three settings are on the page with their outcomes stated, and the test asserts the read-only outcome says "reports".

The six-part anatomy: planner, specialists, tools, boundary, observations, verifier, one line each.

**Dropped, with reasons.** Two of the mockup's four closing truths are now said by the equation and by step 02 of the existing loop, and a reader told the same thing twice trusts the second telling less. The two that were said nowhere are folded in instead: "having a tool is not the same as being allowed to use it" sits under the boundary settings, and "a saved change is not a finished job" closes the scene. The 720x520 system map did not come; it positions every label by absolute offset inside a fixed viewBox, and Phase 5 already recorded what that does at a width nobody tested.

## One collision, caught by an existing guard

The anatomy names `Boundary`, and so does each lane, so the scene now has three `<dt>Boundary</dt>` where a v4.4.1 guard required exactly two. The guard was right about the claim and wrong about the scope: the claim is that both LANES carry matching labels, not that the string appears twice in the document. It now counts within the two lane lists, and asserts there are exactly two of those.
