## What the review asked, and what it removed

"All we want to see and understand is that chatbots can provide responses to a variety of problems,
but do not have the ability to accomplish tasks or modify existing files or projects. Agentic
platforms can. Only keep one segment named Agentic Platforms."

That is one idea, and the scene was carrying two structures to deliver it: a six-stage vertical flow
(entry, planning, three mission lanes, boundary, observations, report) and, in a separate scene, the
two-lane comparison. The flow went. The comparison stayed, because it already showed exactly the
asked-for idea: both lanes get the same request and show the same two zones, and only the reach
differs.

What the platform does beyond answering is now four numbered chips rather than six stacked stages:
plans the job, delegates, acts conditionally, checks and reports. The permission boundary sits under
them, keeping the conditional language the earlier scenes established.

## Eight assertions retired or repointed, all registered

The merge touched more tests than any other phase in this plan family:

- Two scene counts (seven to six) and the title list.
- The comparison module's four tests, which simply followed the markup into its new home.
- The platform choreography test, retired with the flow it measured.
- The shared-grammar rule, which said Models and Agentic Platform must open with a byte-identical
  entry motif. That rule existed so a comparison BETWEEN two scenes would teach. With one scene
  carrying both lanes, the sameness is on screen instead of in the markup, so the rule is recorded
  as retired and replaced by assertions on the merged scene.
- The connector contract, from three roots and seven paths to two and three, because the retired
  flow owned one root and four connectors.

## Two copy rules held rather than loosened

The broad module requires this comparison to say that the difference is `where the work happens` and
`what each lane leaves behind`, and to carry `same request`. The rebuild had dropped all three
phrases while keeping the meaning, so they went back into the copy rather than out of the test.

That pushed the scene to 115 words against its own 100-word ceiling from v4.4.3. The copy was
tightened twice, to 98, rather than the ceiling being moved for a scene that had just absorbed
another one.

## The stale-offset splice, repeated

v4.4.3 Phase 8 recorded this mistake: compute a section's offsets, edit something earlier in the
file, splice with the offsets already computed. This phase made it again. The new stylesheet block
shifted every later index, so the merged scene landed inside a base64 payload and took the Models
scene's closing tags with it.

It was caught by rendering the scene and looking at it: the screenshot showed an image alt text
where a scene should have been. The guide was restored from the last commit and the splice redone
with the spans recomputed.

Writing the lesson in a history file did not prevent the repeat. What prevents it is the recompute
sitting on the line above the splice, in the script itself, which both scripts now carry.
