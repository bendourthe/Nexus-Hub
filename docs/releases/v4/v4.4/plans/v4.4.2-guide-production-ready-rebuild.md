# Plan -- Guide Production-Ready Rebuild

**Project**: Nexus-Hub
**Version**: v4.4.2
**Slug**: guide-production-ready-rebuild
**Plan Type**: Feature / Enhancement (visual, narrative, motion, and arena rebuild from direct review of v4.4.1)
**Created**: 2026-09-02
**Goal**: Ship a production-ready single-file Nexus Hub guide whose Home restores the v4.1.2 hero and its five explanatory sections at a readable scale, whose Foundations page teaches each concept through one coherent title system, balanced layouts, annotated prompt and context examples, and choreographed animations that show a model nested inside a platform harness wrapped by the Nexus Hub harness, and whose Training arena supports pointer play with dense varied threats inside a fullscreen presentation that uses the entire window at every desktop size.

## Overview

This patch responds to a direct visual review of the v4.4.1 build (twelve annotated screenshots). The review found three classes of defect. Home lost its explanatory depth when v4.4.0 rebuilt it: the v4.1.2 hero subtitle and lead, the "Why it matters" cards, the raw-prompting comparison, the command-migration table, and the "Three things" explainer are all gone, and section titles are too small to anchor the page. Foundations has correct content but almost no storytelling motion (the whole file carries three keyframes), inconsistent title styling against Home, two-column scenes that leave one column empty, a prompt example that renders as four separate cards instead of one annotated prompt, misplaced connector arrows, and it dropped the single most useful picture from earlier releases: the model nested inside the platform harness, wrapped by the Nexus Hub harness. Training's arena spawns nothing in four of its eight scenes, has no pointer input, presents touch buttons where a key guide belongs, and its fullscreen slide, while now overlap-free, is sized for safety rather than density and leaves most of a 1920x1080 window empty.

The rebuild keeps the guide's delivery contract unchanged: `guides/website/nexus-hub-guide.html` remains one offline HTML file with no server, build step, CDN, remote font, or runtime network call, under a strict 500,000-byte ceiling. It stands today at 292,339 bytes, so the plan has roughly 200 KB of headroom and allocates it by phase in a measured ledger rather than spending it implicitly. Motion is added through one small declarative sequencer rather than per-scene bespoke scripts, and every animation has a static reduced-motion end state, an offscreen pause, and a hidden-tab pause, because those are existing tested contracts.

Execution is sequenced after v4.4.1 integrates. Do not cut the `feat/v4.4.2-guide-production-ready-rebuild` branch until the v4.4.1 pull request is merged into `develop` and `develop` is refreshed locally; v4.4.1's unpublished Phase 7 evidence and its open `HT-2` installer item belong to that release and are not reopened here. This plan does not authorize any push, pull request, merge, tag, or release before its own final phase.

No `STRATEGY.md`, `docs/solutions/` store, or constitution exists in the checkout, so Goals First is grounded in the user's annotated review, the guide maintainer contract in `guides/website/README.md`, the v4.4.1 evidence block in `docs/releases/v4/v4.4/development/last-phase-evidence.md`, and the open v4.4 known gaps.

## Goals First and Definition of Done

### Target problem

The v4.4.1 guide is correct, offline, and fully tested, but it does not yet look or read like a production website: Home is thin and its headings do not anchor the page, Foundations explains without showing (three keyframes in the whole file), and the Training arena is sparse and its fullscreen slide wastes most of the screen.

### Target personas

- **Primary - newcomer**: a reader without AI or software vocabulary who must understand prompts, context, models, platforms, harnesses, and Nexus Hub from the pictures as much as from the prose, so the pictures have to move and be labelled.
- **Secondary - workshop learner**: a participant who plays the arena with mouse or keyboard, sees the seeded bug, fixes it, adds vertical movement, and presents the result.
- **Secondary - trainer or maintainer**: a presenter who needs a dense fullscreen slide that fills a projector, deterministic demonstrations, offline delivery, and retained evidence.

### User stories

- **US1 - Home depth**: as a newcomer, I read one hero statement and six short sections and understand what Nexus Hub is, why it matters, how it works, why it is safe to let it act, and how my existing commands map onto it, without zooming.
- **US2 - Foundations comprehension**: as a learner, I can watch a prompt and its context flow into a model, watch the platform harness and the Nexus Hub harness shape the output, and I can point at each part of an annotated prompt and name what it is for.
- **US3 - Arena play**: as a workshop participant, I can play the shooter with mouse or keyboard, the game pauses when my pointer leaves it, enemies and asteroids keep coming at varied speeds and sizes, and the seeded bug still happens on cue.
- **US4 - Presentation density**: as a trainer, fullscreen fills the whole window at 1280x720 through 1920x1080 with every region visible and scaled, not centred in empty space.
- **US5 - Safe maintenance**: as a maintainer, I can prove the guide is offline, under budget, accessible, deterministic, responsive, and covered by fail-closed local and integration browser gates, and that no count on the page can drift from the catalog.

### Observable definition of done

- Home shows the exact hero title `Nexus Hub` and, beneath it, the subtitle `Upgrade your agentic AI platforms with an autonomous team of world experts` and the lead paragraph, styled to the v4.1.2 template (centred, gradient span on the closing phrase, lead in muted body colour); the `Trademarks and credits` disclosure is gone from the Home flow while any attribution the asset licence requires survives in a footer.

#### 1.2 -- Declarative motion sequencer `nhg-seq`

**Objective**: One small, shared timeline engine that every animated Foundations scene will use, honouring the guide's existing motion contracts.

**Failure modes**: An element with a malformed `data-seq` value (non-numeric step, missing duration) is skipped with a `console.warn` naming the element id, and the rest of the timeline still runs; if `IntersectionObserver` is unavailable the timeline starts immediately on load rather than never; if a timeline is asked to start while already running (a scroll back into view during playback), the second start is ignored and the running instance completes, then re-arms; if reduced motion is active, `play()` applies the terminal step synchronously and never schedules a frame.

**Build class**: load-bearing.

**Prompt**:
> In `guides/website/nexus-hub-guide.html`, add a `window.NexusSeq` module (target under 3,000 bytes minified-by-hand, plain ES5 like the rest of the file). Contract: a container carries `data-seq-root`; children carry `data-seq="<step>"` and optional `data-seq-dur="<ms>"`; the sequencer adds `is-on` to each step's elements in order, waits the duration, and moves on, looping if the root has `data-seq-loop`. It starts when the root is at least 40 percent visible (`IntersectionObserver`, threshold 0.4), pauses when it leaves the viewport, pauses on `document.visibilitychange` hidden, and resumes on visible. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, `play()` adds `is-on` to every step at once and stops. Expose `NexusSeq.play(root)`, `pause(root)`, `reset(root)`, `state(root)` returning `{step, running, reduced}` for tests. Add the CSS transition primitives the steps use (`.seq-fade`, `.seq-rise`, `.seq-draw` for SVG stroke-dashoffset) in one block with a `prefers-reduced-motion` override that sets every transition duration to 0. Add ONE demonstration root on Home (the workflow loop pills lighting in sequence) so Phase 1 can prove the engine before any Foundations scene depends on it. Write browser tests in a new `tests/guides/test_v442_phase1_foundation.py`: steps advance in order, offscreen pauses, hidden-tab pauses, reduced motion reaches the end state with zero scheduled frames (assert via a counter on `requestAnimationFrame`), and a malformed step is skipped with a warning while the rest run.
