# Comparison -- Self-Improving Harness Loop, Formal State-Machine Checks, and Opus 5.5 Workflows

**Summary**: Gap analysis of five sources (three Opus 5.5 practitioner workflows, a survey of self-optimizing agent stacks with three papers and one repository, a Karpathy post on readable output, an effort-budgeting article, and a Simplified Technical English skill repository) against Nexus-Hub v4.13.8 plus its queue through v4.18.1. Most source material is already shipped or owned by a queued plan. The new value is concentrated in one place: Nexus-Hub's improvement loops verify that a change passed, but never record what the change predicted, so they cannot detect a change that passed for the wrong reason, a fix that was already rejected, or a stack of same-type edits that degrades behavior below the per-case detection threshold.

## Section 1: Executive Summary

Of 27 extracted insights, 2 are shipped, 4 are owned by queued plans (v4.16.1, v4.17.11, v4.18.0, v4.18.1), 11 are partially implemented, 8 are missing, and 2 do not apply. They collapse into eight adoption candidates (C1 to C8, Section 4); three published mechanisms are rejected (Section 8).

The five highest-value candidates, in priority order (C6 to C8, a defaults-taken handoff section, video design notes, and an STE checker, follow in Section 6):

1. **Falsifiable change manifest for the improvement loop (P0, skill-native).** Each candidate change to a skill, hook, or instruction names the cases it should fix, the cases it puts at risk, and a trace signature that must appear if the change actually fired. After the replay, the loop scores how many predictions came true. This is the single mechanism behind three of the papers' defenses: catching a change that passed for the wrong reason (reward hacking), declining to retry a rejected fix, and noticing when proposals stop predicting anything (under-exploration).
2. **Edit-concentration audit (P1, skill-native).** Flag when two of the last three accepted changes to one surface share an edit type and their combined prediction hit rate is under 0.4. Self-Harness and HarnessX both record a stack of individually harmless "reminder" rules that together cost 14 points; Nexus-Hub's instruction templates grow by exactly this kind of accretion.
3. **Difficulty-band rule for eval fixtures (P1, skill-native).** A fixture every run passes, or every run fails, carries no signal. When a baseline sits at 0 or 1, reshape the fixture (start state, allowed actions, or observation) toward a 0.4 to 0.6 pass band instead of adding more fixtures.
4. **State-machine verification skill (P2, skill-native, optional local toolchain).** A procedure to model a retry loop, session, or queue as a state machine, check it, and admit a counterexample only after it reproduces as a failing test in the real code.
5. **Narrowest-surface routing rule (P2, skill-native).** A short decision table that picks which layer to change (skill, harness, environment, orchestration) from the failure's location, added to the existing improvement loop.

## Section 8: Risks, Conflicts, and NOT Recommended (Step 7)

Risks and conflicts:

- **C1 adds record overhead.** A manifest on every candidate is friction. Mitigation: required only for candidates that pass Step 5; rejected candidates keep a one-line reason.
- **Hit rate is noisy on small sets.** HarnessX itself warns that a single-round miss is sampling noise. C2 counts across three accepted changes, never one.
- **Paper gains are not portable claims.** Self-Harness and HarnessX report gains on the sets they tuned against (HarnessX has no held-out split). The adoption is the mechanism, not the numbers; no Nexus-Hub document should quote them as expected gains.
- **C3 can be gamed.** Reshaping fixtures toward a band could make a skill look worse or better at will. The rule therefore forbids grader edits and requires the reshaping to be recorded before measurement.
- **C8 licensing.** S5 is MIT, but its `references/word-list.md` derives from the ASD-STE100 dictionary, which ASD copyrights. v4.18.1 already decided no word table ships; C8 checks only the dictionary-free rules.

NOT recommended:

- **Raven runtime or its SkillForge** (drop-outright): remote installer, third-party agent routing, and an external skill catalog fall in the hard-no class of the MCP Registry Policy. The orchestration ideas are already covered by `agent-orchestration-primitives`.
- **`dashboard-builder` as published** (drop-outright): it writes the global `CLAUDE.md`, which conflicts with lockstep templates, and duplicates the handoff file, the progress dashboard, and the usage monitors. C6 keeps its one new idea.
- **Model-harness co-evolution** (drop-outright): requires training an open-weight model.
- **A four-stage multi-agent evolver** (not recommended): HarnessX's own ablation finds no accuracy gain over a single evolver at this capability level.
- **Springs, beat sync, dual aspect** (backlog): cosmetic until the video capability exists.
- **Voice API narration** (drop-outright): third-party generation-as-service.
