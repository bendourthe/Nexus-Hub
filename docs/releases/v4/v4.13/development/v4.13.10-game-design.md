# v4.13.10 game design: the Nexus Defenders engine (code name Sky Sentinel)

**Covers**: the architecture of the from-scratch game engine on the Training page (plan sub-task 3.1, T010) and, later, the logo boss geometry (sub-task 6.1, T022): simulation and rendering split, seeded random streams, the two defect flags, the public and test interfaces, sizing, pause rules, and the test rules. Revision 3 (R14) adds the start screen, the ships, the upgrade carriers, density growth, the wormhole, and the maps. Revision 4 (R16) makes the start screen fit without scrolling in both games, makes every damage path fatal in the buggy build, and gives each ship its own weapon. Revision 5 (R18) aims every enemy weapon at the ship, centres the end card, adds a fifth ship with one main colour per ship and animated weapon previews, and adds a scripted demo mode. Revision 9 (R33) makes the Talon's lance a pulsed full-height ray, gives every weapon upgrade an effect on every ship, opens the boss's core once its nodes fall, and records the balance band and its measured table. Revision 10 (R35 to R37) gives enemies health that grows with their bulk and with the level, lets the ship fly the whole arena, raises cadence and speed level over level with shields on the last level, adds four downgrades, moves every background layer, and gives the Nexus boss an entrance, a life bar, agent ships, and a finale that hands off to the reward. Revision 12 (R39 and R40) compacts the start screen's pickup key, hides which enemy or rock carries a pickup, makes missiles visible, makes difficulty rise linearly within each level and step up between levels (with larger life bars, shields from level 2, and larger rocks), gives the Nexus boss three shield layers, a glowing last core, rising agent launches, and its own storm background, and replaces the finale with a black hole distinct from the wormhole. Revision 13 (R41 and R42) gives the fixed game one retry per level (the boss fight included) and replaces the short wormhole into the boss arena with a supernova that tears open a wormhole into the Nexus dimension. Revision 14 (R43) turns the Nexus boss's X frame a quarter turn at a time so every node is fairly reachable, keeps every node clear of the boss bar, fixes singular and plural boss text, and adds two rare pickups, the instant Revive and the Atomic blast. Revision 18 (R48) clears the battle away before the new universe, which keeps turning behind an end card that now carries a score summary, and puts the two rare pickups on a schedule of one each per level. Read it before changing the engine, its tests, or the story's use of it.

**Name**: players see the game as "Nexus Defenders" (start screen, arena label, live-region text). The code keeps its working name: `window.SkySentinel`, `guides/website/src/sky-sentinel.js`, the `data-ss-*` attributes, and the `.ss-*` classes, so the page and its tests stay stable.

## Revision 18 (R48, 2026-10-07): a clean new universe, the score summary, and scheduled rare pickups

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ. Tests: `tests/guides/test_training_game_revision18.py`, plus the updated tests listed in the revision 18 history file.

### The clean new universe (T132)

- **The report**: after the black hole, "A New Universe" still showed dozens of the battle's orange shot rings, a leftover blue shield bubble, the hull and shield bars, and the Spread and Rapid chips counting down.
- **The cleanup**: at the finale's flash (and on a skip to the prize card), `cleanUniverse()` removes every player and enemy shot, beam, bomb, and mine, every enemy, rock, and pickup, every explosion, shockwave, and damage number, the running atomic blast, and every timed upgrade and downgrade; the shield and a held Revive go to zero, so no bubble and no chip remain.
- **HUD (decision)**: the new universe hides the fight's HUD entirely (hull and shield bars, chips, retries, goal bar); it shows only "VICTORY" at the top, the map name, and the score. The ship stays hidden: it went through the horizon, and the end card sits where it would be.
- **The scene**: the existing new-universe map (map 5: a teal and rose nebula, a spiral galaxy, two worlds, far galaxies) keeps moving after the finale ends: a calm clock (`S.calm`) drives the drift, the galaxy's turning arms, the worlds' bands (the 2D renderer now draws drifting cloud bands too), and the star twinkle. It shares nothing with the black hole or the wormholes (no horizon, disk, swirl, or streaks). The page keeps animating it only while the arena is on screen in a visible tab (`calmWanted()`); `step(n)` advances it for tests. Reduced motion: one still frame.

### The score summary (T132)

- **Counting (coordinator default)**: every ship, agent, asteroid, and asteroid fragment the game spawns is tagged with its level (the boss fight is level 4, where the megaship counts as one more); destroying a tagged object, by a shot, a collision, or the atomic blast, counts for that level. Objects that leave the arena or fall into a wormhole count as missed. Objects placed by the test layer are not counted.
- **Percentages**: a level's percentage is destroyed over spawned, rounded down so that only a perfect level reads 100 percent; the overall percentage is total destroyed over total spawned across the levels played. Points per level are the score at the level's end minus the score it began with, so the rows add up to the final score.
- **Retries**: a retry reopens its level's counts from zero, as its score already returns to the level's start.
- **The card**: a keyword title ("Victory", or on a loss the existing cause line), "Final score" with the score large, a calm table (Level, Points, Perfect) with one row per level played ("Level 1" to "Level 3", "Nexus Boss") showing the percentage with its counts ("92% 118 of 128"), an "Overall" row, then "Reward unlocked: Nexus AI Studio" on a win, and Play again and Change ship. On a loss after the retries are used, the same table covers the levels reached, the current one marked "so far". The fixed game only; the buggy build and the retry card keep their cards. It fits without a scroll at 1440x900 and 390x844.
- **Hooks**: `scoreSummary()`, `state().calm`, `state().hud.calm`.

### Scheduled rare pickups (T133)

- **No carrier drops**: carriers never hold either rare pickup; `pickDrop` turns their 5 percent back into a Repair (its R13 share), drawing the drops stream exactly as often, so every other upgrade drops as before.
- **The Atomic blast**: exactly one per level, at a seeded moment in the level's last third (`sched` stream, a new stream so no other stream shifts); boss fight: once the first shield layer falls. Its effect is unchanged from revision 14.
- **The Revive**: exactly one per level, at a seeded moment in the level's second half; boss fight: once the megaship drops below half its total health. It is valid only for its level: a held Revive expires when the level ends (`levelUp`, the start of the supernova passage, or the boss's arrival), and its chip disappears. One held at most; a second one in the same level cannot come from the schedule, and if one is caught anyway (the test layer) it repairs.
- **Windows**: the latest spawn leaves 360 ticks before the level's play ends (the level's end, or on level 3 the start of the supernova passage), so it can still be caught. Level 1: blast 3600 to 5040, revive 2700 to 5040; level 2: blast 4400 to 6240, revive 3300 to 6240; level 3: blast 2400 to 2640, revive 1800 to 2640 (ticks within the level).
- **Look and motion**: both enter at the top as free-floating pickups, radius 20 against 14 (1.4 times) and falling 1.08 units a tick against 1.8 (0.6 times). The Atomic blast (`#7cfc3a`) is glowing radioactive material: a green-yellow core hot at its centre, a dark trefoil, and a pulsing halo; the Revive is a gold orb with its rising-ship mark and a ring of sparks. Both renderers draw them (`rare-atomic`, `rare-revive`).
- **Where**: the fixed game with progression and threats on; never in a game with a defect (`dropPowerUp('atomic')` is now refused there too).
- **Start screen**: the details panel reads "Last third of each level" and "2nd half; this level only" (short enough for one line at every size); the full text sits in the title and the API.
- **Hooks**: `rarePlan()`, `spawnRare(kind, x, y)` (test layer), `state()` fields `rareLog`, `expired`, and `pickups`, and the `rarePickup` and `reviveExpired` events.

### Revision 18 balance

Nothing in the weapons, enemies, or boss changed: the harness gives the revision 14 table unchanged (single DPS -11.5 to +15 percent, wave -30.3 to +35.4, hunt mean 64.4 s with -26.1 to +22.1, power index 0.967 to 1.081, smallest upgrade effect +28 percent). The dodging bot (seeds 1 to 20, no retries) reaches the boss on 19 of 20, as before (seed 6 still dies at 208.7 s); it does not seek pickups, yet 14 runs caught at least one rare pickup (14 Revives, 9 blasts), one Revive was used (seed 11), and 13 Revives expired unused at their level's end.

## Revision 14 (R43, 2026-10-07): reachable boss nodes, the Revive, and the Atomic blast

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ. Tests: `tests/guides/test_training_game_revision14.py`, plus the updated key-count and collection tests listed in the revision 14 history file.

### Reachable nodes (T125)

- **The report**: the last nodes at the top of the megaship were almost impossible to hit. From below, the side plates covered the upper nodes except along a 17-unit strip at their outer edge (about 10 world units), and while the lower nodes lived even that strip sat behind them. The megaship also sat a quarter of the arena down, so its top nodes ran under the boss bar.
- **The turn (decision)**: the megaship turns its whole X frame (plates, blades, bars, nodes) a quarter turn at a time: it rests 330 ticks (5.5 s), then turns over 90 ticks (1.5 s) with easing (`BOSS_TURN`). Each turn moves every node one corner round, so each node spends half the fight in the lower half, where straight fire from the player's zone reaches it. While no live node sits in the lower half it rests only 60 ticks, and it turns the way that brings more live nodes down (a tie keeps its last way), so the last top node comes down within about 2.5 s. Once every node is down it settles on a half turn, with its plates at the sides again, so the core stays open from below. A rotation was chosen over moving the nodes or shrinking the armour because it keeps the plates and blades guarding as before (while a node lives, a round at the core glances off and a plate absorbs what strikes it, at any angle) and makes the change readable: the player sees the frame turn a node toward them.
- **Clear of the bar (decision)**: the megaship sits a quarter of the arena down, or lower when its frame would reach into the boss bar's band (the 84 CSS px band the ship's own top limit already clears, 142 on a phone), and it dips a little while it turns, since a node reaches highest halfway through a quarter turn. No node rim ever enters the band: at 1440x900 the megaship rests at y = 197 of 600 (it was 150), at 390x844 at about 400 of 960 (it was 240).
- **Rendering**: both renderers turn the hull, the nodes, and their glows with the frame (`bossOff`, `bossPoint`, `bossLocal`); the finale's wreck keeps the angle the fight ended on.
- **Measured reach**: `game_balance.node_reach` counts, over 60 s of the node stage (seed 7, shields broken, sampled every 5 ticks), the share of ticks in which a straight upward round (radius 4) from below meets each live node first along an open strip at least 12 world units wide. The floor is 35 percent (`NODE_REACH_MIN`).

| Nodes already down | Node 0 (top left) | Node 1 (top right) | Node 2 (bottom left) | Node 3 (bottom right) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 50 percent | 50 percent | 57 percent | 57 percent |
| the two lower nodes | 74 to 75 percent | 80 percent | - | - |
| all but node 1 | - | 100 percent | - | - |
| all but node 0 | 98 to 100 percent | - | - | - |

  The same figures hold at 1280x900 and 390x844; before R43 the two top nodes had no strip that wide while a lower node lived. The least gap between a node rim and the bar's band over the node stage is 6 world units at both sizes.
- **Text**: every boss count now reads correctly for one: "Break the last glowing node, then the core", "Shields down: break the last node", "Inner shield: the last of 3 layers", and on the live region "1 shield layer left" and "One node left: break the last node". Counts above one are unchanged ("Break the 3 glowing nodes, then the core"). A `count(n, one, many)` helper builds the shield count; the engine sweep found no other count string that can read "1 ...s" (the timed pickups all last 8 s or more, and the retry note is a label).
- **Hooks**: `bossGeometry()` gains `rot`, `quarter`, `turning`, `nodeR`, and `band`; `state().boss` gains `rot`, `quarter`, `turns`, and `blasted`; `nodeReach(r)` gives each node's open x span.

### The Revive (T126)

- **What it does (coordinator default)**: a Revive is held until needed, one at a time (`#fde047`, a small ship rising inside a halo). When the hull empties with a Revive held, the ship bursts and is rebuilt on the spot with a full hull and 120 ticks (2 s) of invulnerability, and the level goes on. Nothing near it is cleared; the invulnerability is the protection. The shield bar is not restored.
- **Before the retry**: the held Revive answers the loss first, so the level's retry stays unspent; a second loss on the level then offers the retry card as before.
- **One at a time (decision)**: a second Revive picked up while one is held repairs 35 hull instead, so a pickup is never wasted.
- **Fixed game only (decision)**: a game with a defect switched on never holds one: `dropPowerUp('revive')` refuses it and its share of the drops comes out as a Repair, so the buggy build drops exactly what it dropped before. The start screen's key stays the same in both games (as R16 requires), so it lists the Revive in the buggy game too.
- **The look**: a white-gold burst (14 ticks), a gold ring closing in on the ship while it grows back from a spark (36 ticks), then a gold shimmer ring and the usual blink until the protection ends; "REVIVED" rises over the ship. A gold "Revive HELD" chip leads the chip row while one is held, and the DOM HUD lists "revive held". Reduced motion: the ship is drawn whole and the burst and the ring hold still for the 36 ticks.
- **Hooks**: `state()` fields `revive`, `reviveT`, `revives`, and `player.look` (the drawn scale), and the `revive` event.

### The Atomic blast (T126)

- **What it does (coordinator default)**: it fires once, immediately on pickup, with no key. A shockwave grows from the ship 16 world units a tick until it has crossed the arena (about 45 to 65 ticks), and its front destroys at once every enemy (the boss fight's agent ships included), every rock (whole, without splitting), and every enemy shot, beam, bomb, and mine it passes; shots and mines simply vanish. Kills score their normal points, and a carrier destroyed by it still drops its pickup.
- **Against the megaship (coordinator default)**: when the front reaches it, the blast breaks the shield layer standing (its full strength, with the usual burst and 500 points), or once the shields are down takes 40 percent of a node's health (30 of 75) from every live node; it never touches the core, so it never kills the megaship. In the core stage it only rings off the hull.
- **The look**: a white flash over the arena at the start (16 ticks), then a crisp white-hot front over a faint fill with an orange heat band behind it, a second ring that wavers, and 48 glints along the edge that flicker in and out (the heat shimmer); it fades over 26 ticks once it has crossed the arena. Both renderers draw it (`atomic-flash`, `atomic-ring`, `atomic-shimmer`). Reduced motion: the front jumps in three still steps (a third, two thirds, then the whole arena), and what it clears always matches what is drawn.
- **Hooks**: `state()` fields `blast` and `blastLog`, the `atomic` and `atomicBoss` events, and `blastPlan()`.

## Revision 13 (R41 and R42, 2026-10-07): one retry per level and the supernova passage

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ. Tests: `tests/guides/test_training_game_revision13.py`.

### One retry per level (R41, T123)

- **Scope (decision)**: retries belong to the fixed game only. A game with either defect switched on (the buggy build, or the fixed game configured with a defect) never offers one, because the buggy build exists to show that its first hit ends the run.
- **Levels**: levels 1, 2, and 3 each grant one retry, and the boss fight counts as a level of its own (key 4 in `state().retries`), so a player can retry level 3 and still have a retry for the boss.
- **The retry card**: the first loss on a level shows a centred card instead of the end card: the title "Ship Lost", the note "Retries left: 0", and a "Retry level" button with the end card's button style (`.ss-retry` shares the `.ss-start` rules). Play again and Change ship stay hidden on it. The run is still `over` (so the explosion plays out and the loss reads as a loss), with `state().retryOffer` set.
- **What a retry restores**: the level from its start: a full hull, the chosen ship, the level's starting difficulty (its curves restart at 0 percent), no upgrades, downgrades, shield, enemies, shots, rocks, or pickups, and the first-shot rules again. On level 3 the boss clock restarts at 60 s; on the boss level the megaship arrives again with its full entrance and its full 1,625 points.
- **Score (decision)**: a retry restores the score the level began with (`state().levelScore`), so points earned in the lost attempt do not count; points from earlier levels are kept. `retryLog` records each retry with the points it gave up.
- **A second loss** on the same level shows the normal end card, whose note also reads "Retries left: 0", and the live region adds "No retries left on" the level.
- **The HUD**: "Retries left: N" in small grey-teal type under the score and the map name, top right (`state().hud.retry`), and the same count in the DOM HUD's level item for screen readers. Once the card offers the retry it counts as spent, so the HUD and the card both read 0.
- **Keyboard and screen readers**: the card moves focus to the Retry level button when focus was on the arena; the button is a native button, so Enter and Space work; Start-game shortcuts (Enter on the arena, a click) take the retry while it is offered. The live region announces "Ship lost: ...; Retry level restarts level N with a full hull" and, on the retry, "Retrying level N with a full hull".
- **Hooks**: `retry()`, `state().retries`, `retriesOn`, `retriesLeft`, `retryOffer`, `retryLog`, `levelScore`, `bossLevel`, the `retry` event, and `retry` and `level` on the `destroyed` event.

### The supernova passage into the Nexus dimension (R42, T124)

When level 3 is cleared, the short wormhole into the boss arena is replaced by a 600-tick (10 s) passage. It starts 600 ticks before the boss is due and ends on the boss's arrival tick, so the boss still arrives 60 s into level 3 (tick 15,600 of a full run); level 3 is now 50 s of play and 10 s of passage, where it was 56.5 s and a 3.5 s wormhole. The test-only "Jump to the boss" keeps its short wormhole.

| Ticks | Stage | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 149 | swell | the Ember Belt's star (in place of the map's sun, at the same spot and size) swells to 1.45 times its radius and flickers faster and harder; what is left of the level backs away from it |
| 150 to 209 | collapse | it falls in on itself to an eighth of its size while it heats from ember to white-blue, and the camera starts to shake |
| 210 to 299 | burst | a white flash spreads from the star across the arena, a filamented white-blue shock ring expands across the whole arena (vaporising every enemy, rock, and pickup it passes, worth nothing) with a faint remnant glowing inside it, and 48 ice-blue knots of ejecta ride out with it |
| 300 to 359 | tear | the shock tears open a wormhole at the star: its mouth, rimmed in violet light, grows from a point and slides to the centre until its tunnel fills the view |
| 360 to 519 | travel | the ship flies through the tunnel, centred and steady (no spin, no shrink), while its violet and teal walls, ribbed and banded, stream past faster and faster toward pale light at the far end |
| 520 to 599 | emerge | the map becomes the Nexus Storm, seen through the tunnel's far end as it widens with a bright rim, and the ship glides down to its start position |
| 600 | | the megaship's entrance begins |

- **Distinct (decision)**: it shares no element with the wormhole between levels or the black-hole finale. Its palette (`NOVA_LOOK`: white-blue star and shock #eef6ff, #b9d4ff, #8fb8ff; ice-blue ejecta #d6e4ff, #7aa2ff; tunnel violet #5b2bd6, teal #12a594, exit #c9fff4, rim #9d7bff) is used nowhere else in the engine. The level wormhole is a bright flat swirl disc with cyan and violet arms on dark space, stars streaked into lines, and a ship that spins and shrinks; the finale has a dark centre, warm colours, bent stars, and a stretched ship. The passage has no disc, no arms, no streaked stars, no dark centre, and its tunnel fills the whole arena with depth. Its frame tags (`nova-star`, `supernova-flash`, `shock-ring`, `ejecta`, `nova-tunnel`, `tunnel-walls`, `nexus-arrival`) are disjoint from both sets in both renderers. Over the whole arena, about 80 percent of a tunnel frame's pixels are violet or teal and none are dark, against about 30 percent and over half dark for a level wormhole frame and under 1 percent for a finale frame.
- **Rendering**: in WebGL the star, the shock ring and remnant, and the tunnel are drawn in the background shader (the tunnel's noise is sampled on the circle, so it has no seam where the angle wraps), with the ejecta, the star's bloom, and the mouth's rim as additive sprites; the flash and the caption ride on the HUD canvas. The 2D renderer draws the same stages with gradients, arcs, and rings rushing outward between twisting ribs.
- **Invulnerable**: the passage is a transition (`S.trans`, kind `nova`), so nothing can hurt the ship, no one fires, and nothing spawns; enemy shots vanish as it starts and no lancer keeps its aiming tell.
- **Skipping**: as in the finale, Space, Enter, Escape, a click, or a tap skips: the first skip lands on the arrival in the Nexus Storm, a second starts the megaship's entrance. Input in the first 45 ticks is ignored (and key repeats always), so a player still tapping fire as level 3 ends does not skip it by accident. `skipNova()` is the test hook.
- **Reduced motion**: a 300-tick passage of five still frames (60 ticks each): the swollen star, the shock ring with its ejecta, the tunnel opening, the ship in the tunnel, and the arrival; the collapse is folded into the first. The boss still arrives on time.
- **Caption and live region**: a caption over a dark band names each stage with the skip keys, the goal line names it too, and the live region announces each stage ("Supernova. The shock wave sweeps the arena.").
- **Hooks**: `novaPlan()`, `skipNova()`, `state().nova` (stage, star, shock, flash, ejecta, tunnel), `state().novaLog`, `transitionInfo.stage`, `state().hud.nova`, and the `novaStage` event.

### Revision 13 balance

Nothing here changes the weapons, the enemies, or the boss: the balance harness (`tests/guides/tools/game_balance.py`) gives the revision 12 table unchanged, and the band holds.

## Revision 12 (R39 and R40, 2026-10-06): a calmer start, hidden carriers, felt difficulty, the shielded boss, and the black-hole finale

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ. Tests: `tests/guides/test_training_game_revision12.py`, plus the updated tests listed in the revision 12 history file.

### The start screen (R39, T118)

- **Compact key (decision)**: the ships panel shows the five ship cards, the selected ship's line, and one strip: a "Pickups" button followed by ten small upgrade icons, a thin divider, and four hazard icons (violet glow). Each icon names itself and its effect in a tooltip and to a screen reader. The upgrade key with names and effects is gone from the ships panel.
- **Pickups** swaps the hangar for the full key (both lists, with names and one-line effects) and becomes "Back to ships". This is the panel switch R16 used on narrow arenas only; it now applies at every size, so the default screen is the same calm layout everywhere.
- The strip is one row at every tested desktop size; at 390 px the button and the icons share one row too. Every tested size (1280x900, 1440x900, 1920x1080, 1366x768, 1280x720, 390x844) fits both panels with no scroll bar.
- The key's wording changed with the carriers: "Upgrades: some enemies and rocks drop one when destroyed; nothing shows which" and "Hazards: some drop one of these instead, so a pickup is a gamble".

### Hidden carriers (R39, T118)

A carrier is drawn exactly like any other enemy of its class or any other rock, in both renderers: no halo, ring, tint, glow, badge, or warning icon. The pickup shows only once it drops, and a dropped upgrade keeps its gem and colour while a dropped hazard keeps its striped capsule. `state().carriers` and `state().hazards` still list carriers for the tests; nothing on screen does. The test renders the same scene with and without two carriers and compares every pixel of the arena and the HUD canvas.

### Visible missiles (R39, T122)

A homing missile is drawn 2.4 times its old size (a body about 22 by 7 world units with red fins), with a bright head (radius 13) and a grey smoke trail of its last 18 positions plus a short hot exhaust, so its homing path shows. `MISSILE_LOOK` holds the look; the trail is drawing data only. Damage (2) and homing are unchanged, and the balance band below still holds. `missileMarks()` reports what the last frame drew.

### Difficulty curves (R39, T119)

Within a level, the spawn rate (enemy waves per second; interceptors arrive in waves of one, two, or three by level), the enemy speed (a factor on every enemy velocity, taken at the moment of each spawn), the enemy cap, and the asteroid rate rise linearly with the level's elapsed time, from the first value of each `DIFFICULTY` pair to the second. Each interval is the inverse of the rate with a symmetric jitter (0.85 to 1.15, rocks 0.8 to 1.2), so the mean follows the line exactly; the old rock pairs are gone. Between levels each start sits above the last level's start, and at or above the last level's top speed: speed rises 30 and 31 percent from start to start, the spawn rate 52 and 48 percent. Before R39 the within-level ramp was steep and the steps small, so a level began easier than the previous level had ended; that was the "constant" feeling the review described.

## Revision 9 (R33, 2026-10-06): weapons and balance

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ.

- **The Talon's lance pulses and reaches the top**: the ray runs from the nose to the top of the arena (`LANCE`), or stops at the first part of the boss it meets (a node, the core, or armour), and that part is what it cuts. While fire is held it is lit for 24 ticks, then dark for 36 (Rapid cuts the dark time to a third, 12 ticks; the Fighter and Sentinel forms by a fifth). It ignites over 3 ticks and fades over 8, and deals no damage while it fades or is dark. While lit it deals 2 to everything it overlaps once every 4 ticks. Letting go of fire ends the pulse but not the dark time, so tapping fire cannot keep it on. `state().weapon.pulse` reports the cycle and `playerShots[].lit` the ray's state.
- **Pierce on the Talon (decision)**: the ray already passes through every enemy and rock, so Pierce overcharges it instead: half again the damage (3 a window) and a ray 2.4 times as wide (hit radius 12, drawn twice as wide in a hotter yellow). It does not burn through the boss's armour. A first version that did let the Talon destroy the boss in 12 s, about a fifth of any other ship's time, so that was dropped.
- **The lance against the boss**: the boss's nodes and core take 0.4 of the lance's damage. An instant ray that stops exactly on the part it touches never misses, and without the factor it ended the fight in about half the time of any other weapon.
- **The boss's core is open once the nodes fall**: the lower blade lay directly under the core and absorbed every straight shot from below, so after the nodes fell only homing orbs, Spread side shots, or a 5 px gap could reach the core, and the Vanguard, Warden, and Talon could not finish the boss at all. The two blades now guard the core only while a node lives; the side plates still block.
- **The boss no longer jumps on arrival**: its sway clock started at the spawn, so on the tick it arrived it snapped up to 200 px sideways. The clock now starts on arrival.
- **Other ships**: the Warden's flak cone is tighter (+/-0.24 rad) and heavier (2 a pellet), every 24 ticks, and reaches about 270 px (speed 10, drag 0.98, 38 ticks), so it now hits mid-range targets and the boss; before, it reached 180 px, could not touch the boss from anywhere it could fly, and hit a mid-range target with one pellet in five. The Specter's orbs hit for 4 every 18 ticks (they hit for 2 every 22, so its single-target damage was 40 percent of the others'); a piercing orb now splits on its first hit and flies on through, where before a piercing orb never split, so Pierce cost the Specter its split. The Raptor's next pull waits for the burst to finish, and under Rapid the burst's rounds come 2 ticks apart instead of 3; before, a Rapid pull cut each burst short at the Sentinel form, so Rapid added a third instead of doubling the rate. Missiles hit for 2 and also seek the boss's live nodes. Ship speeds: Talon 1.3 to 1.2 (still the fastest), Raptor 1.05 to 1.12.
- **Cards and previews**: the Talon's card reads "A full-height ray that pulses", and its preview shows the ray from the nose to the top of the frame, through the drone, lit for 0.4 s and dark for 0.6 s with the same ignite and fade. The Warden's preview cone now reaches the drone before it fades.
- **Shield, Repair, Time slow, Magnet** do not touch the weapon and work the same on every ship; their existing tests cover them.

### Revision 9 (R33) balance

The harness is `tests/guides/tools/game_balance.py` (run `python tests/guides/tools/game_balance.py`). It drives the real engine with seed 7 and `step(n)` only, against balance dummies (`spawnDummy`: pinned targets that never fire or die), reading damage from `state().dealt`. For every ship, with no upgrade and with each weapon upgrade, it measures:

- **Single DPS**: sustained damage per second against one target 170 px above the ship, over 5 s after a 1 s warm-up (Scout form).
- **Wave DPS**: damage per second against ten targets in five columns 60 px apart and two rows, 130 px and 230 px above the ship.
- **Reach**: the furthest distance above the ship at which the weapon still deals damage, in 20 px steps.
- **Parked boss DPS and parked kill**: the ship held still at the front of its zone on the centre line the boss sways across, firing, immune (Sentinel form, timed upgrades renewed every 5 s). From below, the armour leaves the two upper nodes open only along a narrow outer strip, so a ship that never moves finishes the boss only by chance; these values show which weapons reach past the shielded core and are reported, not banded.
- **Hunt kill**: seconds to destroy the boss with a simple pilot that stays at the front of its zone, slides under the lowest live node, then lines its outermost gun up on the open outer edge of an upper node (at a 0, 12, or 24 px standoff, keeping the best of the three, so homing rounds get room to curve in), then goes under the core. Ship speed counts here.

**The band** (`BAND` in the harness, checked by the tests):

- Single-target DPS with no upgrade within 20 percent of the five-ship mean.
- Wave DPS within 40 percent of its mean, because coverage is where the weapons differ by design.
- Hunt kill time within 35 percent of its mean.
- Each ship's power index within 10 percent of 1. The index is the mean of single DPS, wave DPS, reach, boss speed (1 / hunt), hull, and speed, each divided by the five-ship mean, so a strength on one axis must be paid for on another.
- Every weapon upgrade (Twin, Spread, Rapid, Pierce, Missiles, Wingman) moves at least one measured value on every ship by at least 15 percent.

**Result**:

- Single DPS: mean 13.6, range -11.5 to +15 percent.
- Wave DPS: mean 19.8, range -30 to +35 percent.
- Hunt kill: mean 7.3 s, range -22 to +24 percent.
- Power index: Vanguard 0.99, Warden 1.03, Specter 0.97, Talon 1.01, Raptor 1.01.
- Smallest upgrade effect on any ship: Wingman on the Warden, +28 percent wave DPS.

## Simulation and rendering

| Concern | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Fixed timestep of 60 ticks per simulated second. Real time only decides how many ticks run per animation frame (at most 5, so a slow frame never causes a catch-up spiral). | A seeded run is then identical on any machine, which is what makes the defects testable. |
| Bounds | Every timing rule is in ticks: the start grace (180 ticks), the earliest random explosion (1,200 ticks, 20 s), the minimum gap between explosions (900 ticks, 15 s), and invulnerability after a hit (120 ticks). | Wall-clock timing breaks under a slow CI runner and cannot be fast-forwarded. |
| Randomness | Three independent mulberry32 streams derived from one seed: `spawn`, `drops`, `defects`. The simulation never calls `Math.random` or reads the clock. | Adding an enemy type or a power-up changes the spawn or drop sequence but never shifts when the random explosion happens. |
| World | Logical units, fixed per run: 960 x 600 (landscape) or 720 x 960 (portrait, chosen when the host is taller than it is wide). | Rendering scales the world to the canvas, so a resize rescales without touching state. |
| Rendering | One canvas sized to the content width with device-pixel-ratio scaling; the height follows the world's aspect, capped so the whole play area fits the viewport. Phase 3 draws simple shapes; Phase 6 replaces the painters. | One canvas keeps the hit-test, layering, and resize logic in one place. A layered-canvas design was weighed and rejected: its only gain is cheaper star-field redraws, which this scene does not need. |
| HUD | Score, lives, level, and state live in HTML next to the canvas, with a polite live region for life lost, game over, and level up. | Screen readers and tests read text, not pixels. |

## Entities

Player (position, lives, invulnerability ticks, shield hits, weapon and its ticks, form), enemies (three types in Phase 6; one in Phase 3), enemy shots, player shots, asteroids, power-ups, effects, and the boss (Phase 6).

## The two defects

Defects are independent flags, `{ firstHitFatal, randomExplosion }`. "Fixed" means neither is set.

- **`firstHitFatal`**: the first enemy hit (an enemy shot, an enemy, or an asteroid) destroys the ship outright, whatever its lives. The run ends.
- **`randomExplosion`**: hits cost one life as they should, but the ship sometimes explodes with no hit. The schedule is a pure function of the seed's `defects` stream: the first explosion falls at tick 1,200 or later, each next one at least 900 ticks after the previous, and none while the ship is already exploding or respawning. A due explosion is deferred until the ship is back. Each explosion costs a life.
- **Neither**: a hit costs one life and starts 120 ticks of invulnerability; zero lives ends the run.

Changing the flags mid-run takes effect at the next hit or the next scheduled tick, never retroactively.

## Fairness

No enemy fires during the first 180 ticks. The first enemy shot leaves from an enemy at least 300 world units above the player.

## Pause rules

The game pauses, and releases every held key, when the arena scrolls below 30 percent visibility, the tab is hidden, the window loses focus, or the reader presses Escape. It never resumes by itself; the reader resumes with a click, Enter, or the Resume button.

## Test rules

- Every engine test drives the simulation only through `step(n)` with a fixed seed and `SkySentinel.manual(true)`; no test waits on real time to move the game.
- Tab visibility is tested by overriding `document.visibilityState` and dispatching `visibilitychange`.
- Touch is tested in a browser context created with `hasTouch: true`.
- Many-seed properties (the explosion bounds) run in one `page.evaluate` loop over the pure scheduler, not one browser round trip per seed.
