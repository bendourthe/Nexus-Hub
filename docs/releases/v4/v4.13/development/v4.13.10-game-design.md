# v4.13.10 game design: the Nexus Defenders engine (code name Sky Sentinel)

**Covers**: the architecture of the from-scratch game engine on the Training page (plan sub-task 3.1, T010) and, later, the logo boss geometry (sub-task 6.1, T022): simulation and rendering split, seeded random streams, the two defect flags, the public and test interfaces, sizing, pause rules, and the test rules. Revision 3 (R14) adds the start screen, the ships, the upgrade carriers, density growth, the wormhole, and the maps. Revision 4 (R16) makes the start screen fit without scrolling in both games, makes every damage path fatal in the buggy build, and gives each ship its own weapon. Revision 5 (R18) aims every enemy weapon at the ship, centres the end card, adds a fifth ship with one main colour per ship and animated weapon previews, and adds a scripted demo mode. Revision 9 (R33) makes the Talon's lance a pulsed full-height ray, gives every weapon upgrade an effect on every ship, opens the boss's core once its nodes fall, and records the balance band and its measured table. Revision 10 (R35 to R37) gives enemies health that grows with their bulk and with the level, lets the ship fly the whole arena, raises cadence and speed level over level with shields on the last level, adds four downgrades, moves every background layer, and gives the Nexus boss an entrance, a life bar, agent ships, and a finale that hands off to the reward. Read it before changing the engine, its tests, or the story's use of it.

**Name**: players see the game as "Nexus Defenders" (start screen, arena label, live-region text). The code keeps its working name: `window.SkySentinel`, `guides/website/src/sky-sentinel.js`, the `data-ss-*` attributes, and the `.ss-*` classes, so the page and its tests stay stable.

## Revision 10 (R35 to R37, 2026-10-06): health, movement, difficulty, downgrades, and the finale

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ. Tests: `tests/guides/test_training_game_revision10.py` and the R35 block of `tests/guides/test_training_progression.py`.

### Enemy health and life bars (R35, T110)

Health grows with an enemy's bulk (its radius over its cruise speed) and with the level (DIFFICULTY `hp`). Only the interceptor, the smallest and fastest class, dies to a single hit. Every other class has more health than the heaviest single round any ship fires (a plasma orb, 4, with any upgrade) and more than a Warden flak volley lands at mid range (three pellets, 6), so no ship kills it with one trigger pull at the balance harness's 170 px.

| Class | Radius | Cruise speed | Bulk | Health L1 | Health L2 | Health L3 | Life bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interceptor | 14 | 2.6 | 5.4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | none |
| Agent ship (boss fight) | 15 | 1.4 | 10.7 | 7 | 7 | 7 | yes |
| Gunship | 22 | 1.0 | 22.0 | 7 | 9 | 10 | yes |
| Lancer | 20 | 0.9 | 22.2 | 8 | 10 | 11 | yes |
| Bomber | 28 | 0.6 | 46.7 | 12 | 15 | 17 | yes |

- **Visibility (decision)**: the life bar is always shown, not only after the first hit, so a reader sees at a glance how tough each enemy is before choosing a target. It sits above the enemy, green, then amber under 60 percent, then red under 30 percent, with one notch per point up to 12. A carrier's badge moves up to make room. The balance dummies have no bar.
- **Shields**: a shield ring absorbs whole hits until 5 damage breaks it (any overflow is lost), then the hull takes them. It draws as a pulsing blue bubble with a thin blue bar above the life bar, and flashes white when struck.
- `state().foes` lists every enemy's class, health, and shield; `lifeBars()` returns what the last frame drew; `enemyClasses()` returns the table above.

### Full-arena movement (R35, T111)

The ship flies the whole arena. Its highest point is the HUD band (66 CSS px, 98 on a phone; 128 on a phone while the boss's life bar shows) converted to world units in `fit()`, plus the ship's radius and 4, so its nose never hides under the hull bars, the goal line, or the boss bar. `state().player.top` reports the limit. The first-shot rule still holds: no enemy fires first from less than 300 px above the ship, so a ship parked at the top simply delays the first shot.

Measured over seeds 11, 23, 37, and 41, 200 s per level with the ship firing and repaired. The first-shot grace (180 ticks) and the aimed fire are unchanged. The two misses die late in level 3 after a dozen ordinary hits, with no downgrade involved: the game is harder, as asked.

### Downgrades (R36, T113)

A share of carriers hold a hazard instead of an upgrade. A hazard carrier wears a doubled violet and red ring and an amber warning badge; a downgrade pickup is a dark capsule with a striped violet and red rim and the same badge. The magnet never pulls a downgrade, and `view()` lists a downgrade pickup among the threats, not the prizes.

| Downgrade | Effect | Timer | Drop weight among hazards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mine | A drifting mine that bursts on contact for 20 (scaled by level); shooting it bursts it, harmless unless the ship is within 46 px | burns out after 8 s; the HUD chip counts down the nearest mine | 0.40 |
| Freeze | The ship cannot move | 2.5 s | 0.20 |
| Slow fire | Twice the time between trigger pulls (and the lance's dark time) | 5 s | 0.25 |
| Scramble | Left and right swap | 4 s | 0.15 |

**Drop rates**: an enemy is a carrier 34 percent of the time and a rock 40 percent (26 and 30 before R36); a quarter of carriers hold a hazard. That keeps upgrades dropping about as often as in R33 (25.5 percent of enemies against 26) with hazards on top (8.5 percent of enemies, 10 percent of rocks). An agent ship is a carrier 45 percent of the time, and 45 percent of those hold a hazard. Over the 12,000-tick runs per level above, upgrades and hazards dropped 19 and 2 times on level 1, 31 and 7 on level 2, and 40 and 9 on level 3, so hazards stay a risk, not a wall. Raising the carrier rates was the fix for a real regression: with hazards taking a quarter of the carriers and tougher enemies dying less often, repairs dropped too rarely and the dodging bot bled out on level 3.

Each running downgrade has a hazard chip in the HUD (red and violet rim, warning badge, timer bar, seconds) and a small hazard badge over the ship. The start screen's key lists the four under "Hazards", in the same grid as the upgrades, and still fits every tested size with no scroll bar.

### The moving background (R36, T114)

`bgMotion()` drives both renderers, and `background()` exposes it. Every layer loops: the three star layers scroll at 0.12, 0.32, and 0.75 px a tick; the nebula drifts as an endless noise field in WebGL (sway plus 0.1 px a tick) and as two slow loops in 2D; the planet, sun, or station sways on a slow orbit (6 percent of the width, 4.5 percent of the height, periods of about 44 and 62 s); the station turns; planet bands flow. The old one-way planet drift (0.004 px a tick, never looping) is gone. Reduced motion slows all of it to a fifth.

### The Nexus boss (R37, T115)

- **Entrance**: the megaship arrives through a rift at its station: the rift opens, the ship grows out of it with a camera shake at 45 percent of the way, and a title card reads "NEXUS", "THE NEXUS HUB MEGASHIP", and its goal. It lasts 270 ticks (4.5 s); nothing can hurt the player, and shots pass through the megaship, until it ends. The boss still spawns on the same tick (15,600, or 150 after "Jump to the boss").
- **Life bar**: a large bar across the top of the arena, labelled NEXUS, totals the node armour (4 x 8, cyan) and the core (24, white), 56 in all, with notches per node.
- **Agent ships**: from 150 ticks after the entrance, two agents launch from live nodes (or the core) every 300 ticks, up to six at once, only while threats are on (so the balance harness still measures the boss alone). An agent is a dark saucer with a small glowing Nexus mark: it fans out, brakes, weaves toward the ship's column while it sinks, and fires one aimed dart (8 damage, scaled) every 150 to 220 ticks. 45 percent carry a pickup.
- The node and core logic, the armour, and the kill-time band are unchanged; see the balance table below.

### The finale and the hand-off (R37, T116)

When the core falls, `bossDefeated` fires and the run ends as a victory at once (score, `victory`, and `state` as before), and the finale plays (480 ticks, 8 s):

| Ticks | Phase | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 185 | explode | chain blasts across the scorched, flickering hull every 6 ticks, growing; energy cracks lengthen out of the core; the camera shakes |
| 186 | blast | the final blast: a white flash, a fireball, two shockwave rings (300 and 190 px), shards |
| 210 to 249 | portal | a violet and gold portal opens above the ship |
| 250 to 329 | enter | the ship is pulled in, shrinking and spinning, while stars streak into the portal |
| 330 to 379 | dimension | a flash, and the map becomes "The New Dimension": violet and gold, a golden ringed world, and a star tunnel streaming out of the centre |
| 380 to 480 | prize | the prize card: "REWARD UNLOCKED", "Nexus AI Studio", and "Your download opens below the game" |

On its last frame the game emits `rewardUnlocked`, and the page opens the reward panel on that event (it listened to `bossDefeated` before R37). Space, Enter, Escape, a click, or a tap skips: the first skip jumps to the fully shown prize card, a second ends the finale at once; neither restarts the game. The end card waits for the finale, then shows "The Nexus megaship is down" with a line naming the reward. Under reduced motion the entrance is 90 ticks of one still frame, and the finale is three still frames (the burning wreck, the ship at the portal, the prize card) over 230 ticks. `step(n)` advances the finale while it plays; `skipFinale()` is the test hook.

## Revision 9 (R33, 2026-10-06): weapons and balance

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ.

- **The Talon's lance pulses and reaches the top**: the ray runs from the nose to the top of the arena (`LANCE`), or stops at the first part of the boss it meets (a node, the core, or armour), and that part is what it cuts. While fire is held it is lit for 24 ticks, then dark for 36 (Rapid cuts the dark time to a third, 12 ticks; the Fighter and Sentinel forms by a fifth). It ignites over 3 ticks and fades over 8, and deals no damage while it fades or is dark. While lit it deals 2 to everything it overlaps once every 4 ticks. Letting go of fire ends the pulse but not the dark time, so tapping fire cannot keep it on. `state().weapon.pulse` reports the cycle and `playerShots[].lit` the ray's state.
- **Pierce on the Talon (decision)**: the ray already passes through every enemy and rock, so Pierce overcharges it instead: half again the damage (3 a window) and a ray 2.4 times as wide (hit radius 12, drawn twice as wide in a hotter yellow). It does not burn through the boss's armour. A first version that did let the Talon destroy the boss in 12 s, about a fifth of any other ship's time, so that was dropped.
- **The lance against the boss**: the boss's nodes and core take 0.4 of the lance's damage. An instant ray that stops exactly on the part it touches never misses, and without the factor it ended the fight in about half the time of any other weapon.
- **The boss's core is open once the nodes fall**: the lower blade lay directly under the core and absorbed every straight shot from below, so after the nodes fell only homing orbs, Spread side shots, or a 5 px gap could reach the core, and the Vanguard, Warden, and Talon could not finish the boss at all. The two blades now guard the core only while a node lives; the side plates still block.
- **The boss no longer jumps on arrival**: its sway clock started at the spawn, so on the tick it arrived it snapped up to 200 px sideways. The clock now starts on arrival.
- **Other ships**: the Warden's flak cone is tighter (+/-0.24 rad) and heavier (2 a pellet), every 24 ticks, and reaches about 270 px (speed 10, drag 0.98, 38 ticks), so it now hits mid-range targets and the boss; before, it reached 180 px, could not touch the boss from anywhere it could fly, and hit a mid-range target with one pellet in five. The Specter's orbs hit for 4 every 18 ticks (they hit for 2 every 22, so its single-target damage was 40 percent of the others'); a piercing orb now splits on its first hit and flies on through, where before a piercing orb never split, so Pierce cost the Specter its split. The Raptor's next pull waits for the burst to finish, and under Rapid the burst's rounds come 2 ticks apart instead of 3; before, a Rapid pull cut each burst short at the Sentinel form, so Rapid added a third instead of doubling the rate. Missiles hit for 2 and also seek the boss's live nodes. Ship speeds: Talon 1.3 to 1.2 (still the fastest), Raptor 1.05 to 1.12.
- **Cards and previews**: the Talon's card reads "A full-height ray that pulses", and its preview shows the ray from the nose to the top of the frame, through the drone, lit for 0.4 s and dark for 0.6 s with the same ignite and fade. The Warden's preview cone now reaches the drone before it fades.
- **Shield, Repair, Time slow, Magnet** do not touch the weapon and work the same on every ship; their existing tests cover them.

### Revision 9 (R33) balance

The harness is `tests/guides/tools/game_balance.py` (run `python tests/guides/tools/game_balance.py`). It drives the real engine with seed 7 and `step(n)` only, against balance dummies (`spawnDummy`: pinned targets that never fire or die), reading damage from `state().dealt`. For every ship, with no upgrade and with each weapon upgrade, it measures:

- **Single DPS**: sustained damage per second against one target 170 px above the ship, over 5 s after a 1 s warm-up (Scout form).
- **Wave DPS**: damage per second against ten targets in five columns 60 px apart and two rows, 130 px and 230 px above the ship.
- **Reach**: the furthest distance above the ship at which the weapon still deals damage, in 20 px steps.
- **Parked boss DPS and parked kill**: the ship held still at the front of its zone on the centre line the boss sways across, firing, immune (Sentinel form, timed upgrades renewed every 5 s). From below, the armour leaves the two upper nodes open only along a narrow outer strip, so a ship that never moves finishes the boss only by chance; these values show which weapons reach past the shielded core and are reported, not banded.
- **Hunt kill**: seconds to destroy the boss with a simple pilot that stays at the front of its zone, slides under the lowest live node, then lines its outermost gun up on the open outer edge of an upper node (at a 0, 12, or 24 px standoff, keeping the best of the three, so homing rounds get room to curve in), then goes under the core. Ship speed counts here.

**The band** (`BAND` in the harness, checked by the tests):

- Single-target DPS with no upgrade within 20 percent of the five-ship mean.
- Wave DPS within 40 percent of its mean, because coverage is where the weapons differ by design.
- Hunt kill time within 35 percent of its mean.
- Each ship's power index within 10 percent of 1. The index is the mean of single DPS, wave DPS, reach, boss speed (1 / hunt), hull, and speed, each divided by the five-ship mean, so a strength on one axis must be paid for on another.
- Every weapon upgrade (Twin, Spread, Rapid, Pierce, Missiles, Wingman) moves at least one measured value on every ship by at least 15 percent.

**Result**:

- Single DPS: mean 13.6, range -11.5 to +15 percent.
- Wave DPS: mean 19.8, range -30 to +35 percent.
- Hunt kill: mean 7.3 s, range -22 to +24 percent.
- Power index: Vanguard 0.99, Warden 1.03, Specter 0.97, Talon 1.01, Raptor 1.01.
- Smallest upgrade effect on any ship: Wingman on the Warden, +28 percent wave DPS.

The trade-offs:

- The Warden has the strongest wave and a mid-range punch, paid for with the shortest reach (300 px against 500).
- The Specter has the heaviest hull and homing orbs, paid for with the slowest ship and the lowest single DPS.
- The Talon has the fastest ship and a full-height ray that cuts a whole column, paid for with the lightest hull, the pulse, and the boss's armour factor.
- The Vanguard and the Raptor trade a narrow wave for full reach and steady single-target damage.

## Revision 5 (R18, 2026-10-05): what changed

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ.

- **Centred end card**: the game-over and victory cards sit in the middle of the arena at every size, in both games (`.ss-overlay[data-mode=over] .ss-panel { margin: auto }`), over a soft radial shade instead of the old top band. The damage number stays readable: when a popup would fall under the card, `drawHud()` draws it just below the card (or just above it when there is no room). `state().popupBoxes` reports where each number was drawn, in CSS pixels relative to the canvas.
- **Aimed enemy fire**: every enemy weapon now aims at the ship's position at the moment it fires. Gunship bolts (speed 6, one per gun, two guns at Hard) and interceptor darts (two, speed 7.6) fly straight at the ship; a lancer due to fire first slides onto the ship's column (3.2 px a tick), snaps to it, then holds still through its 50-tick charge, so the beam lands where the ship was when it lined up; a bomb flies to the ship's position and arms there or near the ship (the old wall clamp is gone); the boss's nodes aim at the ship and its exposed core fans five bolts centred on it. There is no lead: a ship that keeps moving escapes, a ship that stops is hit. Difficulty per level still comes from fire rate, shot speed, damage, and the start grace, never from deliberate misses. `aimLog()` returns the last 200 shots with their origin, heading, the ship's position when they fired, and (for bombs) the aim point, so a test can measure the aim error of every class.

## Simulation and rendering

| Concern | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Fixed timestep of 60 ticks per simulated second. Real time only decides how many ticks run per animation frame (at most 5, so a slow frame never causes a catch-up spiral). | A seeded run is then identical on any machine, which is what makes the defects testable. |
| Bounds | Every timing rule is in ticks: the start grace (180 ticks), the earliest random explosion (1,200 ticks, 20 s), the minimum gap between explosions (900 ticks, 15 s), and invulnerability after a hit (120 ticks). | Wall-clock timing breaks under a slow CI runner and cannot be fast-forwarded. |
| Randomness | Three independent mulberry32 streams derived from one seed: `spawn`, `drops`, `defects`. The simulation never calls `Math.random` or reads the clock. | Adding an enemy type or a power-up changes the spawn or drop sequence but never shifts when the random explosion happens. |
| World | Logical units, fixed per run: 960 x 600 (landscape) or 720 x 960 (portrait, chosen when the host is taller than it is wide). | Rendering scales the world to the canvas, so a resize rescales without touching state. |
| Rendering | One canvas sized to the content width with device-pixel-ratio scaling; the height follows the world's aspect, capped so the whole play area fits the viewport. Phase 3 draws simple shapes; Phase 6 replaces the painters. | One canvas keeps the hit-test, layering, and resize logic in one place. A layered-canvas design was weighed and rejected: its only gain is cheaper star-field redraws, which this scene does not need. |
| HUD | Score, lives, level, and state live in HTML next to the canvas, with a polite live region for life lost, game over, and level up. | Screen readers and tests read text, not pixels. |

## Entities

Player (position, lives, invulnerability ticks, shield hits, weapon and its ticks, form), enemies (three types in Phase 6; one in Phase 3), enemy shots, player shots, asteroids, power-ups, effects, and the boss (Phase 6).

## The two defects

Defects are independent flags, `{ firstHitFatal, randomExplosion }`. "Fixed" means neither is set.

- **`firstHitFatal`**: the first enemy hit (an enemy shot, an enemy, or an asteroid) destroys the ship outright, whatever its lives. The run ends.
- **`randomExplosion`**: hits cost one life as they should, but the ship sometimes explodes with no hit. The schedule is a pure function of the seed's `defects` stream: the first explosion falls at tick 1,200 or later, each next one at least 900 ticks after the previous, and none while the ship is already exploding or respawning. A due explosion is deferred until the ship is back. Each explosion costs a life.
- **Neither**: a hit costs one life and starts 120 ticks of invulnerability; zero lives ends the run.

Changing the flags mid-run takes effect at the next hit or the next scheduled tick, never retroactively.

## Fairness

No enemy fires during the first 180 ticks. The first enemy shot leaves from an enemy at least 300 world units above the player.

## Interfaces

```text
SkySentinel.create(host, { id, defects, level, seed, autoStart })  -> instance
instance.configure({ defects, level, seed })   reset with new settings (unknown defect names are rejected with a console warning and no change)
instance.start() / pause(reason) / resume() / reset()
instance.on(event, fn)   events: start, hit, lifeLost, explode, destroyed, levelUp, bossDefeated, pause, resume
SkySentinel.get(id)      the instance with that id

Test layer (window.SkySentinel):
SkySentinel.manual(true)            stop the animation loop so only step() advances time
instance.step(n)                    run n ticks synchronously
instance.input({ left, right, up, down, fire })   hold or release controls
instance.state()                    a plain-object snapshot
instance.configure({ threats: false })   no enemies or asteroids, so the defect schedule shows alone
instance.forceHit(source)           apply one hit now, through the same damage rules
SkySentinel.defectSchedule(seed, ticks, deadTicks?)   the pure explosion scheduler
```

The story drives the engine only through `configure`, `start`, `pause`, and events. Tests use the test layer.

## Input

Keyboard: arrows or WASD move, Space fires (held for auto-fire), Escape pauses, Enter starts. Mouse: a click on the arena starts the game, then fires. Touch: on-screen left, right, up, down, and fire buttons, each at least 44 px, shown on coarse pointers. Holding left and right together cancels rather than sticks.

## Pause rules

The game pauses, and releases every held key, when the arena scrolls below 30 percent visibility, the tab is hidden, the window loses focus, or the reader presses Escape. It never resumes by itself; the reader resumes with a click, Enter, or the Resume button.

## Test rules

- Every engine test drives the simulation only through `step(n)` with a fixed seed and `SkySentinel.manual(true)`; no test waits on real time to move the game.
- Tab visibility is tested by overriding `document.visibilityState` and dispatching `visibilitychange`.
- Touch is tested in a browser context created with `hasTouch: true`.
- Many-seed properties (the explosion bounds) run in one `page.evaluate` loop over the pure scheduler, not one browser round trip per seed.

## Levels and pacing (Phase 6)

Progression and the boss are switched on per game: the buggy game has neither (so the first bug stays the focus), the partly fixed game has progression, and the fixed game has both.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Level 2 (Fighter) | after 5,400 ticks (90 s) of survival |
| Level 3 (Sentinel) | after 6,600 more ticks (200 s from the start) |
| Boss | 3,600 ticks into level 3: 15,600 ticks, 4 min 20 s from the start; 150 ticks after "Jump to the boss" |
| Ship forms | Scout fires one shot every 10 ticks; Fighter every 8; Sentinel fires twin shots every 8. The hitbox never grows, so a bigger ship is not an easier target. |
| Enemies | drone (straight, light aim), weaver (sinusoidal, aimed), lancer (two hits, a three-shot burst; level 2 and later) |
| Power-ups | 14 percent of destroyed enemies drop one, from the `drops` stream: shield (absorbs one hit), twin shot (600 ticks; spread for the Sentinel), extra ship (lives capped at 5) |

Levels advance on survival time rather than score, so the time to the boss is known in advance and does not depend on how well a reader shoots.
