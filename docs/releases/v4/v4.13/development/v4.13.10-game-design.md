# v4.13.10 game design: the Nexus Defenders engine (code name Sky Sentinel)

**Covers**: the architecture of the from-scratch game engine on the Training page (plan sub-task 3.1, T010) and, later, the logo boss geometry (sub-task 6.1, T022): simulation and rendering split, seeded random streams, the two defect flags, the public and test interfaces, sizing, pause rules, and the test rules. Revision 3 (R14) adds the start screen, the ships, the upgrade carriers, density growth, the wormhole, and the maps. Revision 4 (R16) makes the start screen fit without scrolling in both games, makes every damage path fatal in the buggy build, and gives each ship its own weapon. Revision 5 (R18) aims every enemy weapon at the ship, centres the end card, adds a fifth ship with one main colour per ship and animated weapon previews, and adds a scripted demo mode. Read it before changing the engine, its tests, or the story's use of it.

**Name**: players see the game as "Nexus Defenders" (start screen, arena label, live-region text). The code keeps its working name: `window.SkySentinel`, `guides/website/src/sky-sentinel.js`, the `data-ss-*` attributes, and the `.ss-*` classes, so the page and its tests stay stable.

**Plan**: [v4.13.10-training-page-rebuild](../plans/v4.13.10-training-page-rebuild.md)

## Revision 5 (R18, 2026-10-05): what changed

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ.

- **Centred end card**: the game-over and victory cards sit in the middle of the arena at every size, in both games (`.ss-overlay[data-mode=over] .ss-panel { margin: auto }`), over a soft radial shade instead of the old top band. The damage number stays readable: when a popup would fall under the card, `drawHud()` draws it just below the card (or just above it when there is no room). `state().popupBoxes` reports where each number was drawn, in CSS pixels relative to the canvas.
- **Aimed enemy fire**: every enemy weapon now aims at the ship's position at the moment it fires. Gunship bolts (speed 6, one per gun, two guns at Hard) and interceptor darts (two, speed 7.6) fly straight at the ship; a lancer due to fire first slides onto the ship's column (3.2 px a tick), snaps to it, then holds still through its 50-tick charge, so the beam lands where the ship was when it lined up; a bomb flies to the ship's position and arms there or near the ship (the old wall clamp is gone); the boss's nodes aim at the ship and its exposed core fans five bolts centred on it. There is no lead: a ship that keeps moving escapes, a ship that stops is hit. Difficulty per level still comes from fire rate, shot speed, damage, and the start grace, never from deliberate misses. `aimLog()` returns the last 200 shots with their origin, heading, the ship's position when they fired, and (for bombs) the aim point, so a test can measure the aim error of every class.
- **Five ships, five colours**: Vanguard blue (twin cannons), Warden green (new), Specter orange (plasma orbs), Talon yellow (lance beam), and Raptor red (burst rifle), in that order on the start screen. Each ship's wing panels, edges, drive, card accent, and rounds wear its colour; `ships()` reports `colour` and `weaponColor`. The Warden is a broad flying wing between two tail booms with twin tails (an outline unlike the others), 110 hull, 0.95 speed, 0.9 fire factor, and fires **flak scatter**: each pull sends a cone of five pellets (+/-0.42 rad, speed 9, 1 damage each) that slow and fade out after 24 ticks, about 200 px. Twin doubles the cones; a Spread side barrel adds one pellet; Pierce lets pellets pass through.
- **Animated weapon previews**: each card's picture is a live 240 x 150 scene: the ship (a cached lit sprite, nose up) fires its weapon at a small drone, which flashes in the weapon's colour when hit. Cannons fire paired rounds, flak spreads and fades short of the drone, orbs curve in and split, the lance holds a ray through the drone, and the rifle fires three-round bursts. Each scene is a pure function of time with a few shapes, so five cards cost little; one loop per game draws them only while the start screen shows, the arena is on screen, the tab is visible, and (on a narrow arena) the ships panel is open. With reduced motion every card shows one still frame of its weapon firing. `previewing()` reports whether the loop runs. On an arena under 600 px the five cards sit three over two.
- **Demo mode** (`SkySentinel.demo(host, { variant, ship, seed, renderer })`): a scripted, non-interactive scene drawn by the real renderer (WebGL, or the 2D fallback) at the host's size, with no start screen, buttons, or input, not listed by `ids()` or `get()`. A gunship glides in and, at tick 50, fires one aimed bolt at the parked ship; it lands at about 1.93 s for the demo's `amount` (R19: default 6, the story's hit). `buggy` turns on the first-hit defect, so the ship is destroyed outright (big explosion, the small in-canvas hull bar drains from 100 to 0, the "-6" holds); `fixed` takes the 6 (hull 100 to 94 for the Vanguard, or the chosen ship's hull minus 6) and flies on, lines up, and shoots the gunship down. The scene lasts 4 s (240 ticks) and is a pure function of its tick. The controller is `{ play(), pause(), reset(), seek(ms), on('hit' | 'end', fn), state(), destroy(), info, duration, variant, ship }`; `info` is `{ source: "shot", sourceText, amount, hitMs, hullBefore, hullAfter, destroyed }`, computed by a silent dry run. `hit` carries the source, amount, resulting health, and whether it was fatal; `end` carries `destroyed` and `health`. A demo pauses while its host is off screen or the tab is hidden, and under reduced motion shows its final frame.
- **Demo amount and stepping (R19)**: `SkySentinel.demo(host, { ..., amount })` sets the scripted hit's damage (a whole number, default 6, matching the story, the review, and the Training opening's label), and a forward `seek()` steps on from the current tick instead of replaying, so the Training opening can seek both demos every frame from one timeline and each `hit` and `end` event fires once.
- **Tests**: per class, three shots fired while the ship moves between them each pass within 0.5 px of the ship's position at firing time (beam column and bomb aim point included); a bolt and a dart from 300 px to the side hit a parked ship; the boss's bolts are aimed; the death and victory cards are centred within 2 px at 1440 x 900, 1280 x 720, and 390 x 844 in both games with the damage number visible and uncovered; the start screen fits with five cards at the six sizes; previews animate, differ per card, and stop while playing or off screen, with still frames under reduced motion; the demos fire `hit` then `end`, end destroyed at 0 (buggy) or alive at 90 (fixed), render with WebGL in Chromium, seek deterministically, honour the ship and renderer options, pause off screen, and show the final frame under reduced motion. Because aimed fire now finds a ship that never moves, the dodging bot sweeps each threat forward in time against its own path for nine candidate moves (it still reaches the boss on seeds 1 and 7, taking damage on the way), and two measurement tests that only hold fire keep the ship alive with repair drops.

## Revision 4 (R16, 2026-10-05): what changed

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ.

- **One start screen, no scroll bar**: both games show the same screen: the brand, four ship cards, a one-line ship note, the upgrade key, Start, and the controls hint. The overlay is `overflow: hidden` and is a size container (`container: ss-arena / size`); every size on the screen (card previews, fonts, meters, key icons, gaps) is a `clamp()` of the arena's own height and width (`cqh`, `cqw`), so the layout fits by construction from a 1920 x 1080 page down to a 1280 x 720 one. Under 600 px of arena width the cards form a 2 x 2 grid and the screen has two panels, ships and the key, switched by an "Upgrade key" / "Ships" button, so nothing hides behind a scroll. `fitPanel()` is the safety net: if the panel is still larger than the arena it is scaled down and centred. The sticky Start button that covered the stats is gone. Each key entry is an icon, a name, and a short effect on one line (`POWER_LOOK[k][3]`); the full effect stays in `upgrades()` and the entry's title.
- **Why a hit did not destroy the buggy ship (root cause)**: two paths let visible damage leave the ship alive. First, the buggy game also carries `randomExplosion`; a player who dodged past 20 s saw the self-test blast hit the ship with a "-40" over it and survive, and that blast also opened the 60-tick invulnerability window, during which real hits were ignored. Second, the ship's hit test was an 18 px circle while the drawn wings reach 30 px at the first form and about 45 px at the third, so a bolt or rock could visibly strike a wing and pass through. Now, with `firstHitFatal`, `hitPlayer` ignores the invulnerability window (the wormhole stays damage-free), the asteroid check no longer waits on it, and the self-test blast goes through the same `destroyNow()` as every hit (reason `explode`, text "The ship blew itself up, with no hit"); alone, `randomExplosion` still costs 40 through the shield. Every player collision now tests an ellipse as wide as 72 percent of the drawn half wingspan and as tall as the hit radius (`hitsShip`), reported as `view().player.hw` and `state().hitHalfWidth`. The boss's body sits above the ship's movement band, so its collision damage stays reachable only through `forceHit`; its bolts are a real-loop source.
- **A weapon per ship**: Vanguard fires twin cannons (two heavy cyan rounds, 2 damage each); Raptor a burst rifle (three red rounds, three ticks apart, per pull); Talon a lance beam (a violet ray 230 px long, re-cut every pull while the trigger is held, that hits every target in its reach once per pull); Specter plasma orbs (crimson orbs that steer toward the nearest target ahead and split into two smaller orbs on impact). Upgrades stack on every weapon: Twin (and the Sentinel form) doubles the barrels, Spread adds two angled side shots (four with Twin), Rapid halves the cooldown, and Pierce lets every round pass through (and stretches the lance to the top of the arena). Each card names its weapon and what it does; `ships()` reports `weapon`, `weaponName`, and `weaponNote`, and `state()` reports `weapon` and `playerShots`.
- **Ship styles**: Vanguard stays dark steel with cyan edges; Raptor is gunmetal with red panels and now has forward-swept wings and larger canards, so its outline no longer resembles the Vanguard's; Talon is silver and white with violet edges and drive; Specter is charcoal with a crimson crystal canopy and edges.
- **Tests**: the start screen is measured for both games at 1280 x 900, 1440 x 900, 1920 x 1080, 1366 x 768, 1280 x 720, and 390 x 844 (overlay `scrollHeight <= clientHeight`, `scrollWidth <= clientWidth`, every card, key entry, and the Start button inside the overlay, no clipped label); each damage source is placed as a real hazard against a parked ship in the buggy game and stepped through the real loop until the first hit; the buggy game also dies on its first hit after Play again, after a ship change, and through the real Start button for each ship.

## Revision 3 (R14, 2026-10-05): what changed

This section supersedes the sections below where they differ, including Revision 2's choice of keeping the bars full after a fatal hit.

- **Start screen**: the first overlay shows the "Nexus Defenders" title, four ship cards (a `radiogroup` of `radio` buttons: click, tap, or arrow keys; each card's accessible name states its role, note, and stats), a note on the chosen ship, the `.ss-start` button that still launches the game, and (in progression games) the upgrade colour key. Each card shows a lit preview painted once from the ship's 3D mesh by a small software rasteriser (`meshPicture`), so the cards cost no WebGL context. After a run the overlay shows the cause, "Play again", and "Change ship". The HUD is not drawn under the start screen.
- **Ships**: four designs, each with three forms (form 1 at level 1, then scaled up with added parts at levels 2 and 3). All share one builder (`fighter`) with lofted or faceted bodies, stacked plates, a core light, swept wings with emissive edge strips in the ship's accent, fins, pods, and nacelles. The first ship is the default and keeps the reference stats every older test assumes.

| Ship | Look | Hull | Speed | Fire interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vanguard (default) | dark steel, stacked plating, round cyan core light, wide V wings with cyan edges, pods under the wings | 100 | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| Raptor | gunmetal, long pointed nose with canards, twin side nacelles with red vents, red panels | 90 | 1.05 | 0.8 |
| Talon | silver needle with a ribbed spine, crescent wings that sweep forward and droop like talons | 80 | 1.3 | 1.0 |
| Specter | faceted charcoal kite, red crystal canopy, two tall swept fins, red edges and engines | 125 | 0.85 | 1.05 |

- **Upgrades and carriers**: ten kinds (shield, repair, twin, spread, rapid, pierce, missiles, wingman, time slow, magnet), each with its own colour, icon, and one-line effect. Pierce makes each shot pass through every target once; time slow runs the enemy side (ships, their fire, rocks, spawn clocks) on every other tick for 8 s; magnet pulls upgrades within 420 units to the ship for 15 s. Upgrades no longer drop at random from kills: when an enemy (26 percent) or an asteroid (30 percent) spawns, the `drops` stream may make it a carrier. A carrier glows in its upgrade's colour (a breathing rim light on the mesh, a tinted hull, a halo, a ring, and a badge with the icon) and always drops that upgrade when destroyed, including a rammed carrier. Only progression games have carriers. The HUD lists every active timed upgrade with its icon and seconds left, and a pickup names itself over the ship.
- **Density**: `levelProgress` runs from 0 to 1 through each level (to the boss on level 3; through 90 s in the buggy game). The spawn interval falls from 1.3 to 0.58 of the level's pace, the enemy cap rises from cap - 2 to cap + 3, the asteroid interval falls from 1.5 to 0.45 of the level's rate, and past 40 percent rocks can come in pairs. The dodging-bot test still reaches the boss on both seeds.
- **Wormhole**: a level-up still happens on the same tick (levels at 5,400 and 12,000, the boss at 15,600), so every timing above holds; the wormhole plays during the first 210 ticks (3.5 s) of the new level. It opens ahead of the ship, swallows what is left of the level (no score), and flies the ship in while input is ignored and no damage lands (`hitPlayer` and the random explosion both wait); at tick 120 a white flash switches the map and clears the field; the ship then emerges from an exit swirl and the level banner shows. A shorter one (up to 210 ticks, ending as the boss spawns) carries the ship into the boss arena. Star streaks come from a `uWarp` uniform in the background shader; the swirl is sprite mode 6 with its phase in the fractional part of the mode.
- **Maps**: Cyan Reach (level 1, a blue gas giant), Violet Halo (level 2, a ringed planet whose ring passes in front of and behind it), Ember Belt (level 3, a red sun over a scrolling asteroid belt), and Nexus Station (the boss arena, an orbital station ring with spokes, panels, and lit windows). Each has its own nebula colours and star tint, and the 2D renderer draws a simpler version of each set piece.
- **The buggy build's fatal hit**: `firstHitFatal` now applies the hit's real damage as a large "-N" over the ship, empties hull and shield to zero, and drains the bar from its old value in red while the explosion plays out. The end text still names the source ("Destroyed by a single hit: ..."); the live region adds "A 6-point hit emptied a hull of 100. That is the bug."
- **Interfaces**: `instance.ships()`, `instance.chooseShip(id)` (resets an idle or finished game with that ship; during a run it waits for the next), `instance.upgrades()`, and `spawn(type, x, y, carry)` / `spawnAsteroid(size, x, y, vx, vy, carry)` for tests. `state()` adds `ship`, `shipName`, `shipMesh`, `shipSpeed`, `shipFire`, `map`, `mapId`, `transition`, `transitionInfo`, `levelProgress`, `spawnInterval`, `enemyCap`, `rockInterval`, `carriers`, `drops`, `powerUpKinds`, `upgrades`, `popups`, and `player.pierce`, `player.slow`, `player.magnet`; `healthMax` now follows the ship. New event: `transition`. The host carries `data-ss-ship`, and the overlay a `data-mode` of idle, paused, or over.

## Revision 2 (R10, 2026-10-05): what changed

Sections below that mention lives, drones, weavers, orbs, or mines describe the R5 engine; this section supersedes them where they differ.

- **Rendering**: hand-written WebGL 1, no library or image file. Ships, enemies, and asteroids are meshes built in code, lit by key, fill, and rim lights with specular and emissive glass and engines; glows, beams, blast rings, the shield bubble, and explosions are additive sprites. The HUD is a 2D overlay canvas. Without WebGL the 2D drawing runs instead; force it with `data-ss-renderer="2d"`, `create(host, {renderer: "2d"})`, or `window.SKY_SENTINEL_RENDERER = "2d"`. `state().renderer` says which is active.
- **Health**: the hull has 100 health; the shield is a second 50-point bar that takes damage first; the extra-ship power-up is a repair kit (+35, capped at 100); a hit opens a 60-tick invulnerability window. Damage depends on the source (shots, beams, blast falloff, collisions, and asteroid size: large 28, medium 16, small 7) and rises 15 percent at Medium and 30 percent at Hard.
- **Defects**: `firstHitFatal` destroys the ship on any hit from any source, every run, and the end screen names the source. `randomExplosion` keeps its schedule and bounds and deals 40 damage through the shield.
- **Enemies**: gunships (aimed bolts), interceptors (diving swarms with darts), lancers (a visible charge, then a beam down their column), and bombers (bombs that arm near the ship, show their blast radius, and burst; a shot detonates one early). Asteroids split large, medium, small, then dust.
- **Difficulty**: Level 1 Easy, Level 2 Medium, Level 3 Hard, shown in the HUD and on level-up; spawn pace, fire rate, damage, and the enemy cap rise each level. The logo boss is unchanged.
- **Interfaces**: `state()` adds `health`, `healthMax`, `shieldHp`, `shieldMax`, `difficulty`, `lastDamage`, `damageLog`, `renderer`, and drops `lives`; the `lifeLost` event becomes `damage`, plus `asteroidBreak`; `view()` adds beam and blast threats for the test bot.

## Where the code lives

The engine is `guides/website/src/sky-sentinel.js`. `scripts/stamp_guide_shared.py` inlines it into `training.html` between `/* shared:sky-sentinel-js */` markers; the manifest lists it for Training only. `training.html` stays a self-contained offline file, and the engine stays an ordinary source file that can be edited and reviewed on its own.

## Simulation and rendering

| Concern | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Fixed timestep of 60 ticks per simulated second. Real time only decides how many ticks run per animation frame (at most 5, so a slow frame never causes a catch-up spiral). | A seeded run is then identical on any machine, which is what makes the defects testable. |
| Bounds | Every timing rule is in ticks: the start grace (180 ticks), the earliest random explosion (1,200 ticks, 20 s), the minimum gap between explosions (900 ticks, 15 s), and invulnerability after a hit (120 ticks). | Wall-clock timing breaks under a slow CI runner and cannot be fast-forwarded. |
| Randomness | Three independent mulberry32 streams derived from one seed: `spawn`, `drops`, `defects`. The simulation never calls `Math.random` or reads the clock. | Adding an enemy type or a power-up changes the spawn or drop sequence but never shifts when the random explosion happens. |
| World | Logical units, fixed per run: 960 x 600 (landscape) or 720 x 960 (portrait, chosen when the host is taller than it is wide). | Rendering scales the world to the canvas, so a resize rescales without touching state. |
| Rendering | One canvas sized to the content width with device-pixel-ratio scaling; the height follows the world's aspect, capped so the whole play area fits the viewport. Phase 3 draws simple shapes; Phase 6 replaces the painters. | One canvas keeps the hit-test, layering, and resize logic in one place. A layered-canvas design was weighed and rejected: its only gain is cheaper star-field redraws, which this scene does not need. |
| HUD | Score, lives, level, and state live in HTML next to the canvas, with a polite live region for life lost, game over, and level up. | Screen readers and tests read text, not pixels. |

## Entities

Player (position, lives, invulnerability ticks, shield hits, weapon and its ticks, form), enemies (three types in Phase 6; one in Phase 3), enemy shots, player shots, asteroids, power-ups, effects, and the boss (Phase 6).

## The two defects

Defects are independent flags, `{ firstHitFatal, randomExplosion }`. "Fixed" means neither is set.

- **`firstHitFatal`**: the first enemy hit (an enemy shot, an enemy, or an asteroid) destroys the ship outright, whatever its lives. The run ends.
- **`randomExplosion`**: hits cost one life as they should, but the ship sometimes explodes with no hit. The schedule is a pure function of the seed's `defects` stream: the first explosion falls at tick 1,200 or later, each next one at least 900 ticks after the previous, and none while the ship is already exploding or respawning. A due explosion is deferred until the ship is back. Each explosion costs a life.
- **Neither**: a hit costs one life and starts 120 ticks of invulnerability; zero lives ends the run.

Changing the flags mid-run takes effect at the next hit or the next scheduled tick, never retroactively.

## Fairness

No enemy fires during the first 180 ticks. The first enemy shot leaves from an enemy at least 300 world units above the player.

## Interfaces

```text
SkySentinel.create(host, { id, defects, level, seed, autoStart })  -> instance
instance.configure({ defects, level, seed })   reset with new settings (unknown defect names are rejected with a console warning and no change)
instance.start() / pause(reason) / resume() / reset()
instance.on(event, fn)   events: start, hit, lifeLost, explode, destroyed, levelUp, bossDefeated, pause, resume
SkySentinel.get(id)      the instance with that id

Test layer (window.SkySentinel):
SkySentinel.manual(true)            stop the animation loop so only step() advances time
instance.step(n)                    run n ticks synchronously
instance.input({ left, right, up, down, fire })   hold or release controls
instance.state()                    a plain-object snapshot
instance.configure({ threats: false })   no enemies or asteroids, so the defect schedule shows alone
instance.forceHit(source)           apply one hit now, through the same damage rules
SkySentinel.defectSchedule(seed, ticks, deadTicks?)   the pure explosion scheduler
```

The story drives the engine only through `configure`, `start`, `pause`, and events. Tests use the test layer.

## Input

Keyboard: arrows or WASD move, Space fires (held for auto-fire), Escape pauses, Enter starts. Mouse: a click on the arena starts the game, then fires. Touch: on-screen left, right, up, down, and fire buttons, each at least 44 px, shown on coarse pointers. Holding left and right together cancels rather than sticks.

## Pause rules

The game pauses, and releases every held key, when the arena scrolls below 30 percent visibility, the tab is hidden, the window loses focus, or the reader presses Escape. It never resumes by itself; the reader resumes with a click, Enter, or the Resume button.

## Test rules

- Every engine test drives the simulation only through `step(n)` with a fixed seed and `SkySentinel.manual(true)`; no test waits on real time to move the game.
- Tab visibility is tested by overriding `document.visibilityState` and dispatching `visibilitychange`.
- Touch is tested in a browser context created with `hasTouch: true`.
- Many-seed properties (the explosion bounds) run in one `page.evaluate` loop over the pure scheduler, not one browser round trip per seed.

## Levels and pacing (Phase 6)

Progression and the boss are switched on per game: the buggy game has neither (so the first bug stays the focus), the partly fixed game has progression, and the fixed game has both.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Level 2 (Fighter) | after 5,400 ticks (90 s) of survival |
| Level 3 (Sentinel) | after 6,600 more ticks (200 s from the start) |
| Boss | 3,600 ticks into level 3: 15,600 ticks, 4 min 20 s from the start; 150 ticks after "Jump to the boss" |
| Ship forms | Scout fires one shot every 10 ticks; Fighter every 8; Sentinel fires twin shots every 8. The hitbox never grows, so a bigger ship is not an easier target. |
| Enemies | drone (straight, light aim), weaver (sinusoidal, aimed), lancer (two hits, a three-shot burst; level 2 and later) |
| Power-ups | 14 percent of destroyed enemies drop one, from the `drops` stream: shield (absorbs one hit), twin shot (600 ticks; spread for the Sentinel), extra ship (lives capped at 5) |

Levels advance on survival time rather than score, so the time to the boss is known in advance and does not depend on how well a reader shoots.

## The logo boss (Phase 6, sub-task 6.1)

The plan expected to trace the logo PNG by hand. That turned out to be unnecessary: the guide already carries an exact vector of the mark, the shared `nexus-mark` symbol in `guides/website/shared/nexus-mark.svg.html`. The boss uses its coordinates in their own 512-unit space, scaled by 0.62 (landscape) or 0.5 (portrait):

| Part | Geometry (512-unit space) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Bars | (106, 97) to (410, 395) and (410, 97) to (106, 395), width 26 | absorb shots |
| End nodes | radius 34 at the four bar ends | turrets: 8 hits each, aimed fire every 84 ticks |
| Core | radius 30 at (258, 246) | shielded until every node falls, then 24 hits; fires a five-shot spread |
| Side plates | (89,144)-(196,250)-(89,356) and (425,144)-(320,250)-(425,355) | absorb shots |
| Blades | (164,102)-(285,179)-(270,196) and (352,398)-(231,321)-(246,304) | absorb shots |

Defeating the core fires `bossDefeated`, adds 5,000 points, and ends the run as a victory. A headless capture at 1280 px confirmed the drawn ship reads as the Nexus Hub mark.
