# Plan -- Training Page Rebuild: a Live Nexus-Loop Demo on Its Own Page

**Project**: Nexus-Hub

**Slug**: training-page-rebuild
**Filename**: `v4.13.10-training-page-rebuild.md`
**Plan Type**: Feature
**Created**: 2026-10-03
**Status**: queued
**Goal**: The guide's Training tab opens a separate, offline `guides/website/training.html` that walks a reader through the whole Nexus loop as a live agentic coding demo: a full-width, code-drawn game with two seeded bugs, power-ups, three levels, and a logo megaship boss, a stage tracker tied to the home page's loop, structured agent sessions that show the model tier, effort, and mapped model at each stage, and page openings and clickable outlines that match the guide's other pages.

## Overview

The current Training page (built by v4.13.4) teaches the Nexus loop with a small, left-aligned arcade game and short simulated agent replies. On 2026-10-03 the maintainer reviewed it with screenshots and found it not ready to ship: large empty areas and uneven spacing, basic graphics in half the width, a first enemy shot that is nearly unavoidable, no progression or power-ups, no visible link back to the home page's loop, plain-text agent output with cramped scrolling code panels, and current steps that do not stand out. The maintainer also asked that every content page open the same way (a clear title, a description, and a visual) with a clickable outline, and that the final fixed game end with a boss shaped like the Nexus Hub logo.

This plan moves Training to its own offline page, because the guide is one file with a 500,000-byte release ceiling and only about 12 KB of headroom at authoring time; moving Training out also frees roughly 105 KB for the other pages. The game is rewritten from scratch and drawn entirely in code (no image files), playable on desktop and phone. The story walks the loop twice: the first loop fixes a bug that destroys the ship on the first enemy hit, then `/review` finds a second, hidden bug (the ship sometimes explodes with no hit) and a shorter loop fixes and ships it. Every stage shows what a real agent would choose, on a badge that stays visible: the tier, the effort, and the model the tier maps to today, switchable between the four providers in Nexus-Hub's bundled model map.

The build order protects the lesson: the page shell and openings come first, then a playable engine with simple shapes, then the full story and sessions, then the checks, and only then the art, power-ups, levels, and boss. The old in-guide Training stays live until the new page is complete; the cutover (switching the tab, redirecting links, removing the old page, retargeting tests) is its own phase near the end. If time runs short, the cut-line below drops polish first and never the lesson.

## Definition of Done

**Must ship:**

1. **Separate page.** `guides/website/training.html` works offline from `file://`, loads no external script, font, or image, and stays under its own byte ceiling (400,000 bytes, LF-normalised). The guide's Training tab and every former Training link open it, old guide hashes (`#training`, `#training/<section>`) redirect to the matching stage, and the guide no longer contains the old Training markup, scripts, or styles. When `training.html` is missing beside the guide, the Training tab shows a short notice explaining how to get it.
2. **Main guide.** `nexus-hub-guide.html` stays under its 500,000-byte ceiling with three in-page pages (Home, Foundations, Cheatsheets) plus the external Training tab; Previous/Next, the progress dots, and arrow-key paging skip the external tab.
3. **Consistent openings and outlines.** Foundations, Training, and Cheatsheets open with the same component (title, one-paragraph description, visual summary) and carry a clickable outline that highlights the current section, works by keyboard, and collapses on phones.
4. **Training intro.** Training opens by explaining that it walks the whole Nexus loop with a game, and shows a visual summary of every stage before the first one starts.
5. **Loop tracker.** The home page's six stages (describe, review, plan, implement, test, update) appear through Training and light the stage the reader is on in both loops; in the second loop `/describe` shows as "already mapped".
6. **Fair, playable game.** A from-scratch, code-drawn game spans the full content width, plays with keyboard, mouse, and touch, and fires no enemy shot in the first 3 seconds after start.
7. **Two bugs, two loops.** The buggy build destroys the ship on the first enemy hit and sometimes explodes it with no hit (never before 20 seconds of play, never twice within 15 seconds). A callout before play names the first bug and says one more hides in the build. Loop one fixes the first bug; `/review` then names and proves the random explosion; loop two fixes and ships it.
8. **Agent sessions.** Each session renders as a structured report (headings, lists, code blocks, a diff) with a file panel at least half the content width at 1280 px that needs no scroll bar, except a diff longer than 40 lines, which scrolls inside its own block. Each stage's model badge (tier, effort, mapped model, one-line reason) is always visible; a closed-by-default "Show technical details" disclosure holds command flags and file paths only.
9. **Stamped models.** Badges' mapped models are stamped from `catalog/skills/ai-development/model-routing/references/last-known-model-map.json` for all four providers; a check fails when the page and the map disagree or a badge names an unknown tier or provider.
10. **Density.** At 1280 px and 390 px in both themes, no section contains an empty rectangle at least 25 percent of its content width wide and 160 px tall (1280 px), or at least 50 percent wide and 120 px tall (390 px), measured from the rendered layout on an 8 px grid; the current v4.13.4 Training fails this measurement in all seven sections at 1280 px (recorded in Phase 1; the plan's first vertical-band measure missed the horizontal emptiness and was corrected there).
11. **Tests.** Old in-guide Training tests are retired or retargeted, new browser tests cover the page, engine, story, tracker, outline, stamping, and density, and the CI guide job runs them within its time budget.
12. **Maintainer review and the v4.13 close.** The maintainer reviews the fully implemented page on desktop and phone before release. On acceptance, every open v4.13 gap migrates to `docs/releases/v4/v4.14/known-gaps.md` and `docs/releases/v4/v4.13/` is archived after the release; on a change request, the owning phase reopens and v4.13 stays open.

**Ships after the lesson (cut in this order if time runs short; each keeps a minimal form where a maintainer decision requires one):**

13. **Game richness.** Power-ups (a shield, an extra weapon, an extra ship), three levels, and the player's ship transforming at each level.
14. **Logo boss.** On level 3 of the fixed game, a boss drawn in code as the Nexus Hub logo; the minimal form is the drawn logo ship with one weak point, the full form adds turret nodes and armor plates. A "jump to the boss" option appears after the story.
15. **Polish.** Detailed drawn art (three enemy types, shaded asteroids, parallax background), the progressive typing animation, "Run again", and the action timeline.

**Out of scope:** the home page's content (beyond reusing its loop), the Cheatsheets' command content (already current), Foundations' body content, real (non-simulated) agent calls, and audio.

**Pacing targets:** reading the whole story without playing takes under 15 minutes; a typical player reaches the boss in 4 to 5 minutes of play.

## Phase 3: Game engine core

**Goal**: A from-scratch, deterministic engine that fills the content width, carries both defects as independent flags, and exposes clean interfaces to the story and to tests, drawn with simple shapes.
**Prerequisites**: Phase 2.
**Stability Gate**: The engine runs full width on desktop and phone with keyboard, mouse, and touch; no enemy shot fires in the first 3 seconds; each defect flag reproduces exactly its bug; a seeded run is identical every time.
**Verification Expectation**: Open `training.html#play-buggy`, start, and observe no shot for 3 seconds and then the ship destroyed by the first enemy hit; through the test API, enable only `randomExplosion`, fast-forward with `step(n)`, and observe an explosion with no hit after 20 seconds of simulated play; clear both flags and observe one hit costing one life. Exercise through [[functional-verification]].
**Recommended model tier**: frontier
**Recommended effort level**: high
**Rationale**: A new real-time engine with determinism, three input sources, and exactly reproducible defects is the plan's highest technical risk.

### Sub-tasks

#### 3.1 -- decision: engine architecture

**Objective**: Fix the engine's shape so the story and the art build on stable seams.

**Prompt**:
> Record in `docs/releases/v4/v4.13/development/v4.13.10-game-design.md`: a fixed-timestep simulation separated from canvas rendering; independently seeded random streams (`spawn`, `drops`, `defects`) so new content never shifts the defect schedule; timing bounds measured in simulation ticks, not wall-clock time; an entity model (player, enemies, enemy shots, asteroids, power-ups, effects, boss); defects as independent flags `{ firstHitFatal, randomExplosion }`, with "fixed" meaning neither; a public engine interface for the story (`configure({ defects, level, seed })`, `start`, `pause`, and events `hit`, `destroyed`, `levelUp`, `bossDefeated`); `window.SkySentinel` as a thin test layer adding `step(n)`, `state()`, and the defect scheduler as a pure function; canvas sizing to the content width with device-pixel-ratio scaling; pause when off screen, when the tab is hidden, and when focus leaves; the level count (three) and the 4-to-5-minute time-to-boss target. Weigh one canvas versus layered canvases. Test rule: every engine test drives the simulation only through `step(n)` with a fixed seed and never waits on real time; visibility is tested by overriding `document.visibilityState`, touch in a `hasTouch` browser context.

#### 3.2 -- Core loop, input, and sizing

**Objective**: The engine runs, scales, and takes input.
